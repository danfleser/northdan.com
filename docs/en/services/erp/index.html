ERP that fits the company, not the reverse

One system where orders, stock, production and money finally agree with each other.

An ERP project is really a decision about where truth lives in your company. When quantities in the warehouse, prices in sales and costs in accounting disagree, every department keeps its own defensive spreadsheet and management flies blind. We help mid-sized European companies fix that — sometimes by implementing and integrating an established ERP, sometimes by building custom modules around a standard core, and occasionally, where a business is genuinely unusual, by developing a tailored system outright. Our bias is stated openly: the least software that makes your numbers agree. Romanian engineering rates make the economics work, EU contracts and English-language delivery keep the collaboration simple, and migration of your historical data is treated as a first-class part of the project rather than a footnote.

What you get

Selection without vendor spin

We benchmark standard ERPs against your processes before recommending anything — including the option of keeping what you have and integrating around it.

Modules where you are special

Custom production planning or pricing logic can attach to a standard financial core — you pay for uniqueness only where your business is actually unique.

History survives the migration

Years of articles, partners and documents move over cleaned and validated — continuity of data is half the value of the system.

Custom ERP or a product off the market?

Buy when your processes are ordinary in the good sense: standard procurement, standard warehousing, standard accounting. Established products encode decades of other companies’ lessons, and bending your business slightly to fit them is usually cheaper than encoding your current habits into code that you then own forever.

Build, or extend with custom modules, when a process is genuinely your competitive advantage or genuinely unusual — configurable production, complex pricing, an industry the standard vendors have never served properly. The common answer is a hybrid: a standard core for finance and stock, custom modules where you are actually different, and an integration designed so that upgrading the core does not break your part.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an ERP implementation actually take?⌄

For a mid-sized company, six months to a year for the full rollout, including migration and training. A single first module goes live considerably sooner, but anyone quoting six weeks for an entire company is describing a demo install. We phase go-lives by department to keep the business running throughout.

Custom ERP or standard system — what do you honestly recommend?⌄

Standard for finance and HR almost always; custom only where your operational process is a real differentiator. Most of our engagements end as hybrids — a standard core with custom modules at the edges.

Our data is in five places and none agree. Is that a blocker?⌄

It is the reason to start. Reconciling those sources is the first project phase — we resolve conflicts with your team by explicit rules, so the new system opens with numbers everyone has signed off.

Can EU funding cover an ERP implementation?⌄

For companies operating in Romania, yes — digitalisation grants routinely treat ERP licences, implementation services and the related hardware as eligible costs. Eligibility, co-financing rates and deadlines depend on the specific call, so we scope the technical side to fit the programme you are applying under rather than the other way round.
