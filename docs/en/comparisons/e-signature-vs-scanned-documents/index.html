Scanned signature vs electronic signature: what each one is worth

A scanned signature looks like a handwritten one, but it is only an image. Only an advanced or qualified electronic signature can prove who signed and that the text was not changed afterwards.

8
sections
7
questions answered

A scanned signature is an image of your handwritten signature placed in an electronic document: under EU law it counts at most as a simple electronic signature, so it has legal effect; under Romanian law, if it is contested, the party relying on it must prove who signed, whereas a qualified electronic signature has, across the EU, the same effect as a handwritten one. The practice in a great many companies looks identical: the document is printed, signed with a pen, scanned or photographed, and sent by email. The resulting file is an image — it looks convincing, it travels easily, and partners accept it without comment. What it cannot do on its own is prove anything. A picture of a signature does not say who held the pen, does not say when, and cannot show whether a page was swapped after signing. A qualified electronic signature solves exactly those three things: it binds the document to an identity verified by a qualified trust service provider, adds a timestamp, and seals the content so that any later modification is detectable when the file is opened. That does not mean you must sign everything electronically. It means the choice is made on the stakes. For an internal approval note, scanning is sufficient and free; for a high-value contract, for filings to public authorities, or for an archive that has to survive for years, the image gives you little to stand on if it is contested. Checked on 18 November 2026 against the text of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and Romania's Law No 214/2024.

Key takeaways

✓What scanning gives you

Zero cost, zero implementation, wide acceptance in everyday practice, and nothing new for the team to learn. For low-stakes documents and partners you have worked with for years, it remains a reasonable and quick solution.

✓What the electronic signature gives you

The signer's identity verified by a qualified trust service provider, in the qualified variant, an associated timestamp, and the content sealed cryptographically. Verification can be performed by anyone, at any time, simply by opening the file in an ordinary document reader.

✓The operational gain

The print, travel, scan and file routine disappears. A contract can be signed remotely in minutes, and the document goes straight into the electronic archive, indexed and searchable, without passing through a physical folder at all.

✓Where the difference truly shows

In a dispute or an inspection. An electronically sealed document verifies itself; for a scanned image, the burden of proof falls on whoever relies on it. The difference never matters, right up until the day it matters enormously.

What the law says: eIDAS and Romania's Law 214/2024

The EU's eIDAS Regulation (No 910/2014) says, in Article 25, that an electronic signature cannot be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic or not qualified, and that a qualified electronic signature has the legal effect of a handwritten signature. That applies across the EU. Romania's Law No 214/2024 carries the rule over: every type of electronic signature produces legal effects and can be used as evidence in court (Art. 3(1)).

The difference lies in the effects. The three cases below are Romanian law (Law 214/2024, Art. 4(9)); other member states have their own rules. A simple signature, a category that in practice includes a scanned image, has the effect of a handwritten signature only in three cases: the document is worth less than half of Romania's gross minimum monthly wage, the other party acknowledges or performs it, or two companies agreed in writing beforehand to give it that effect. Outside those, the document may be treated as a beginning of written proof (Art. 5(4)).

When someone contests a simple or advanced signature, the party relying on it must prove that the legal and technical conditions are met (Art. 5(3)). For a qualified signature it is the other way round: the burden of proof lies with whoever contests it (Art. 5(1)). That is the practical difference between an image and a certificate.

A paper document signed and scanned vs a signature image pasted into a PDF

These are two different situations that people call by the same name. In the first, the document was printed and signed with a pen, and the scan is only a copy of it: the paper original exists and remains the signed instrument. Keep it, because in a dispute it is the original that counts, not the file sent by email.

In the second, someone scanned their signature once and pastes the image into every PDF. There is no original here: the image can be copied by anyone who ever received a document from you, and the file does not show whether the text changed afterwards. This is the most fragile situation and the most common one.

When a scan is not enough

If the law requires written form as a condition of validity, an electronic document meets it only with a qualified signature or with an advanced one that has handwritten effect (Law 214/2024, Art. 4(3)). If the law requires a notarial deed, you need a notary; the electronic signature law does not apply (Art. 4(11)).

In dealings with the state the rules are fixed. Companies identify themselves in ANAF's Private Virtual Space (SPV) with qualified certificates, and electronic acts issued by authorities are signed with a qualified signature or an advanced one on a certificate issued by a public institution (Art. 24). The Romanian electronic identity card carries a certificate for an advanced signature issued by the Ministry of Internal Affairs (Art. 3(5)), and such a signature has the effect of a handwritten one (Art. 4(5)(a)).

How to split your documents into two columns

Make a simple inventory of the document types you sign each month and place them in two columns. In the first, the low-stakes internal traffic: notes, approvals, meeting minutes, operational confirmations. Scanning stays sufficient there. In the second, everything with consequences: customer and supplier contracts, addenda, documents filed with public authorities, documents for banks or for grant-funded projects, and anything that should stand as evidence five years from now.

For the second column, check the exact form the recipient requires before buying anything. Not every document accepts the same level of signature, and the requirements differ between institutions and document types. In Romania, for instance, filings through the SPV — the online taxpayer portal run by ANAF, the Romanian tax authority — expect a qualified certificate, and the same applies to e-Factura, the country's mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing system. The practical rule: ask in writing what form the other side accepts and, for documents with serious legal implications, confirm with a lawyer.

A framework agreement between companies

If you work often with the same partners and want to keep the speed of scanning, Law 214/2024 gives you a route (Art. 4(9)(c)): two companies can agree, in a document signed by hand or with a qualified signature, that simple electronic signatures between them have the effect of a handwritten one. In the same document, the parties confirm that they understand the risks and the burden of proof. The wording of the clause is for your lawyer to set.

When you do not need us for this

If you sign a handful of documents a month, you need no software project whatsoever: buy a certificate from an accredited provider, install it, and sign straight from your document reader. The cost is small, it is settled in an afternoon, and it involves nobody from outside. Any firm proposing a platform for that volume is selling you infrastructure for a problem you do not have.

It becomes our business when volume and workflow demand automation: hundreds of documents a month, generated from data, routed to several signers in order, with reminders, status tracking and automatic filing into the electronic archive. Then we build the workflow on top of the accredited provider you choose — we connect it to your systems, we do not issue certificates and we do not give legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a scanned signature?

It is an image of your handwritten signature, obtained by scanning or photographing and inserted into an electronic document. It contains no certificate and does not tie the document to your identity. In practice it counts at most as a simple electronic signature: it has legal effect, but it is easy to copy and hard to defend if contested.

Is a scanned signature legally binding?

It has legal effect and can be evidence (eIDAS Art. 25). Under Romania's Law 214/2024 (Art. 4(9)) it equals a handwritten signature only if the document is worth under half of Romania's gross minimum monthly wage, the other party acknowledges or performs it, or two companies agreed so in a document signed by hand or with a qualified signature.

Can I turn a scanned signature into an electronic signature?

No. An image stays an image, however clear. To get an advanced or qualified electronic signature, you sign the document again with a certificate: from a qualified trust service provider or, for the advanced signature, with the certificate on the electronic identity card. The graphic look of the signature has no legal weight.

Is a printed, signed and scanned contract valid?

A contract is valid if the law requires no particular form, because it is formed by the parties' agreement. The problem is proof: if the partner contests the signature, you must show that they signed. Keep the paper original and, for contracts with real stakes, use a qualified signature.

What is the difference between the levels of electronic signature?

Simplified, they rise in the order of the guarantees they carry: from a plain association of identity up to the qualified variety, issued by an accredited provider after verifying who you are. The higher the level, the harder the evidence is to contest, and some documents explicitly demand the qualified form.

Do I need a physical device for a qualified signature?

Classic versions use a cryptographic device, while newer ones work in a provider-hosted form with confirmation on your phone. The choice depends on how often you sign and how many people in the company need signing rights — ask for both quotes before deciding.

How do I store signed documents so they stay verifiable?

Archive them in their original format, not as a reprinted image, together with the validation information. A printed copy or a screenshot of an electronically signed document loses precisely the part that mattered. Decide from the outset where the files are deposited and who holds deletion rights.
