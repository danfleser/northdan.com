WordPress or custom website: which fits your company

WordPress wins on launch cost and self-editing; custom wins when the site must do something specific to your business, or when speed and security cost you sales.

7
sections
6
questions answered

Pick WordPress when the site is mostly content updated by non-technical people and the starting budget is small; pick a custom site when it must do something specific to your business — price calculation, ERP integration, customer accounts — or when speed and security make or lose sales. WordPress runs on 38.5% of all websites and 57.5% of those with a known CMS (W3Techs, November 2026), which makes it the default choice — sometimes rightly, sometimes expensively. WordPress wins on upfront cost and content autonomy: you publish pages without a developer, and a ready-made theme gets you live in days. A custom site wins on freedom: you do not drag along dozens of plugins with their vulnerabilities, you do not depend on updates that break your layout, and you never hit the ceiling of a theme when you need business-specific functionality. Our practical rule, which we apply even when it means sending you elsewhere: content site on a small budget — WordPress; company site meant to win clients, with integrations and performance expectations — custom; application-like functionality — custom, no debate.

Key takeaways

✓WordPress: fast, cheap start

Live in days, content editing without a developer, thousands of themes — right for blogs and simple presences on tight budgets.

✓Custom: speed and security

No third-party plugin code you do not control: speed depends on how the site is built, not on how many plugins pile up, and security depends on who maintains it.

✓The long-term difference

WordPress adds premium-plugin renewals and update checks; custom has none of those, but every functional change needs a developer. Maintaining a presentation site is roughly 50–150 €/month either way.

WordPress vs custom website, criterion by criterion

Upfront cost and launch time: WordPress on a ready-made theme goes live in days and costs less at the start; a custom site needs design and structure built to measure, so more time and a larger starting budget. Price follows project scope, though, not the platform.

Editing content: WordPress ships with a ready admin panel. A custom site gets the same comfort only if it gets a CMS; without one, every changed text goes through a developer.

Speed and security: a WordPress site loaded with plugins slows down and gets exposed; a cleanly written custom site is fast, but not automatically safe — its dependencies need updates just the same. Maintenance matters more than the platform.

SEO: neither wins by platform. Rankings come from content, speed and clean structure; WordPress needs an SEO plugin and plugin discipline, while a custom site must include meta tags, a sitemap and redirects from the start.

What the 2025 security data shows

Patchstack, in its State of WordPress Security in 2026 report published in February 2026, counted 11,334 new vulnerabilities in the WordPress ecosystem in 2025: 91% in plugins, 9% in themes and only 6 in core. 46% had no developer fix when they were disclosed, and the median time to mass exploitation of heavily attacked ones was 5 hours.

The honest conclusion: WordPress core is rarely the problem, plugins are. A custom site avoids that category, but its own code has a framework and dependencies that need updates too. Security means who maintains the site, not just which platform it runs on.

What each costs, at launch and after

Indicatively, excluding VAT, on the Romanian market: an implemented theme (usually WordPress) costs 300–1,500 €; a made-to-order site with its own design and a structure built to measure costs 1,500–5,000 €; dedicated functionality on top of a site, such as client areas, starts from 5,000 €. A made-to-order site can be WordPress with a custom theme or fully custom: price follows scope, not platform.

Long term, maintaining a presentation site is 50–150 €/month, and rare interventions without a subscription are 40–70 €/hour. The final price moves with the number of pages, own design versus a theme, and integrations.

Editing content on a custom website

A custom site does not mean calling a developer for every text. It can get a headless CMS: a separate admin panel, with the site rebuilding on publish. It is the same mechanism we use on Next.js sites.

Before signing, ask in writing which blocks you can edit and which stay in code. Without a CMS, publishing articles depends on a programmer.

Where the balance tips

The balance tips toward WordPress when the site is essentially content and the people updating it are not technical: a blog, a news section, service pages whose wording changes often, and a budget that has to cover design, content and hosting at once. The ecosystem has a plugin for almost anything, and finding somebody to make a small change is easy in any European city.

It tips toward custom when the site starts behaving like software — accounts, calculators, configurators, live integration with an ERP or a booking system — or when performance and security become commercially material because the site is a primary sales channel. The usual tell is plugin count: once a WordPress install needs more than a dozen third-party plugins to do its job, you are paying for fragility rather than saving on development.

Frequently asked questions

Are WordPress sites really more vulnerable?

Yes, through plugins, not core: in 2025 Patchstack counted 11,334 new vulnerabilities in the WordPress ecosystem, 91% in plugins, 9% in themes and only 6 in core. A custom site avoids the plugin category, but needs just as disciplined updates.

Can I start on WordPress and migrate to custom later?

Yes, it is a common and reasonable path: validate the business with a small investment, then migrate when the limits start costing you. Content transfers; themes and plugins do not — so do not postpone until it becomes an emergency.

What do you gain from this comparison?

Nothing from any particular answer: we build both WordPress sites and custom sites, so neither one earns us more. We recommend by what the site must do, and the comparison says explicitly when WordPress is the right choice.

Is WordPress still worth it in 2026?

Yes, for content sites edited in-house. In November 2026 WordPress ran on 38.5% of all websites and 57.5% of those with a known CMS (W3Techs). It stops being worth it when the site must do something specific to your business beyond what plugins cover.

Which is better for SEO, WordPress or custom?

Neither by platform: Google rankings come from content, speed and clean structure. WordPress needs an SEO plugin and plugin discipline; a custom site must include meta tags, a sitemap and redirects. Ask the offer to state exactly what is covered.

Can I edit content myself on a custom website?

Yes, if the site gets a CMS, usually a headless one: a separate admin panel, with the site rebuilding on publish. Without a CMS, every text goes through a developer. Before signing, ask in writing which parts you can edit yourself.
