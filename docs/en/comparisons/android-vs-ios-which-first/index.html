Android or iOS app: which to launch first in Romania

In Romania, Android has the volume and iOS has the audience with more purchasing power — the launch order depends on how your app earns money.

5
sections
5
questions answered

Launch on Android first if your app lives on volume — in October 2026 Android had 73.8% of Romania's mobile web traffic, according to StatCounter — and on iOS first if it lives on subscriptions and in-app payments; on shared code (Flutter, React Native) both stores fit the same budget anyway. If your app targets mass consumption — deliveries, local services, utilities — your audience is overwhelmingly on Android, and shipping on iOS first would mean ignoring almost three quarters of the traffic during the months that matter most. The iOS camp has its own argument: an iPhone costs more, so the iOS audience usually has more purchasing power, and for a premium subscription product the iOS minority can matter more than volume. There is no public per-platform spending statistic for Romania, so you check this against your own data. Cross-platform development has made the question almost irrelevant at code level: you write once and compile for both. At the level of testing, release and marketing the question survives intact — each platform means a separate publishing process, its own test devices and its own promotion budget, and a sequential launch lowers the risk. The verdict: mass-market consumption in Romania — Android first; a premium subscription product — iOS first; a budget that covers both — ship both from one codebase.

Key takeaways

✓Where launching on Android wins

Reach across most of Romania's mobile traffic, publishing on Google Play for a one-off $25 fee against Apple's $99 per year, and volume feedback from the first weeks.

✓Where launching on iOS wins

An audience with more purchasing power (an iPhone costs more) and a homogeneous device base — you test on a handful of iPhone models. Testing across Android's fragmented device range is an indicative 10–15% of the project, which iOS largely avoids.

✓What cross-platform changes

On shared code, publishing to both stores falls in the same MVP band, €6,000–18,000; against two native apps the saving is an indicative 30–40%. The decision stays one of marketing and focus, not of technology.

Deciding on your own product's numbers

The operational question is where the money comes from. An app earning from volume — advertising, small commissions on many transactions, partnerships — belongs where the users are, which in Romania means Android. An app earning from subscriptions or a large basket — premium fitness, professional tools, exclusive services — belongs first where the audience has more purchasing power, which is usually the iOS audience, even though it is almost three times smaller in traffic (26.2% iOS in October 2026, StatCounter). Most business apps, which sell no digital goods inside the app, pay the stores no commission.

Some criteria cut the debate short. B2B apps for field teams often go to Android, because company phones bought one by one are usually Android, being cheaper — count your own fleet rather than assume. Conversely, if your target audience is urban corporate or Western European, the iPhone share climbs above the average. Look at the phones your actual customers carry: the iOS/Android split in your own site's analytics is the best figure for your audience.

Why sequential beats launching both at once

Shipping on two platforms simultaneously doubles precisely the unpredictable part: two approval processes running in parallel, two sets of reviews to manage, platform-specific bugs surfacing at the same moment. The first month after launch is always a run of rapid fixes — with one platform the team handles them with full attention; with two it juggles. We recommend a gap of 4–8 weeks between launches, during which the second version starts out already stabilised.

On budget, on shared Flutter or React Native code the second platform is not a second app: an MVP published to both stores sits in the same band, €6,000–18,000, and what the second launch adds is device testing, platform-specific adjustments and publishing. Written natively (Kotlin, Swift), each platform sits at the top of the band — €9,000–18,000 for Android, €10,000–18,000 for iOS. The exception that justifies a simultaneous launch: marketing campaigns with a publicly announced fixed date, or partners who impose it contractually. Otherwise sequential almost always wins on risk and on quality.

When you need neither app

If the app is an internal tool or its content changes rarely, an installable web app (PWA, €3,500–12,000) skips both stores: no account fees, no review, and every phone runs it from the same code. For the full comparison, see native app versus PWA.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the three-quarters Android figure for Romania?

Nearly exact: in October 2026 StatCounter measured 73.8% Android and 26.2% iOS in Romania's mobile web traffic; over the last 12 months Android ranged between 68.7% and 80.6%. The figure counts page views, not phones sold — for your own audience, your site's analytics data is more precise.

Is publishing on the App Store really harder than on Google Play?

Apple reviews every submission, typically at least 50% in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours, and first-submission rejections are common. On Google Play, personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 need a closed test with at least 12 testers for 14 days before production; organisation accounts skip this step. Budget for one App Store rejection round.

What if I only have budget for one platform for a whole year?

Choose by where the paying audience is and commit fully: one year of focus on a single platform with frequent updates beats two underfunded apps. Build on cross-platform from day one regardless — otherwise next year's expansion means a second app written from scratch, at full price.

Do iOS users in Romania genuinely spend more inside apps?

There is no public per-platform figure for Romania; globally, Sensor Tower shows iOS brought 67% of in-app purchase revenue in Q4 2025, though Google Play leads on downloads. An iPhone's higher price suggests more purchasing power, so a subscription product tests its paid conversion on iOS first.

How much does it cost to launch on both platforms?

On shared code, an MVP published to the App Store and Google Play costs an indicative €6,000–18,000 — the same band as for one platform; on top come $99 per year for Apple and a one-off $25 for Google. Against two separate native apps, shared code saves an indicative 30–40%.
