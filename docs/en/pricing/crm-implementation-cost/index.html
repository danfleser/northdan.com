CRM implementation cost: licences, setup, migration and adoption

Licences are the visible line; configuration, migration, integrations and adoption by the team make up most of a first-year CRM budget.

8
sections
7
questions answered

A CRM implementation costs an indicative €1,000–5,000 for a SaaS CRM configured on your sales process — pipeline setup, contact migration, email and telephony integration and team training — on top of licences that Pipedrive lists at €14–79 per seat per month, billed annually. On paper a CRM can start at zero: Zoho CRM advertises a free edition for three users in the title of its own pricing page, and Bitrix24 advertises a free plan for one to two users on its pricing card — while the FAQ on that same page says user numbers are unlimited on it. The unit of measure differs from vendor to vendor: Bitrix24 prices per organization at €49 per month for five included users (billed annually; Bitrix24 shows prices in the visitor's regional currency, euros from Romania), and Romanian vendor Zarina CRM sells one-off licences from €2,490 (START) to €3,990 (BUSINESS) plus VAT, with unlimited users, on your own server. If your sales process is unusual enough that no existing product fits without painful compromises, a custom CRM starts at an indicative €5,000: €5,000–8,000 for a complete core of customer records, quotes, activities and reports, and €8,000–40,000 for a full system with atypical flows and deep integrations. All our figures are indicative and exclude VAT; what never changes is the rule: a CRM your team does not use costs 100% of the budget and produces zero.

Key takeaways

✓SaaS licences: €14–79/user/month

Pipedrive publishes Lite €14, Growth €39, Premium €59 and Ultimate €79 per seat per month, billed annually — €168–948 per seat per year. Zoho CRM has a free edition for three users; add-ons such as LeadBooster, from €32.50 per month per company, are billed separately.

✓SaaS configuration and training: €1,000–5,000

The pipeline configured on your process, email and telephony integration, plus training for the sales team. Published market estimates for the same segment run higher — implementarecrm.ro quotes €3,000–15,000 for an SME.

✓Custom CRM: from €5,000

The core — customers, quotes, activities, reports — is an indicative €5,000–8,000; a full system runs €8,000–40,000. It makes sense only when your sales process is atypical: complex configurable offers, special approval flows, tight links to production.

✓Data migration: €500–2,000

From another CRM, or from several duplicate-ridden sources, budget an indicative €500–2,000. From one clean spreadsheet it is often included in the setup.

✓Integrations: €0–500 up to €15,000

An e-Factura module in your existing invoicing or ERP software is €0–500; a custom ANAF API integration €1,000–5,000; one API link where no connector exists €500–3,000; a two-way CRM–invoicing sync €3,000–15,000.

CRM implementation cost breakdown, line by line

A CRM is bought in four units that do not compare directly: per user (Pipedrive: €14–79 per seat per month, billed annually), per organization (Bitrix24: Basic €49 per month with 5 users, Standard €99 with 50, Professional €199 with 100, Enterprise from €399, all on annual billing), as a one-off licence on your own server (Zarina CRM: €2,490 START to €3,990 BUSINESS plus VAT, unlimited users), or as a custom project. Zoho CRM sits beside them with a free edition for three users, named in the title of its pricing page; its paid editions are priced per user, and the setup work is the same band whichever SaaS you choose.

Line by line, an indicative budget looks like this. Licences: recurring, for as long as you use the system. Add-ons are billed separately — Pipedrive lists LeadBooster and Smart Docs from €32.50 per month per company, Web Visitors from €41, Projects from €16 and Campaigns from €13.33, so one add-on can cost more than a user seat. Configuration and training: €1,000–5,000, one-off. Data migration: €500–2,000 (own section below). Integrations: €0–500 for an e-Factura module, €500–3,000 for one API link where no connector exists, €3,000–15,000 for a two-way CRM–invoicing sync. A custom CRM has no per-user licence but carries maintenance of 15–20% of the build cost a year, from €300 per month, plus hosting. All figures exclude VAT.

First-year cost for an 8-person and a 20-person team

A concrete case: a B2B services firm with 8 sales agents picks Pipedrive's entry plan at €14 per user per month, billed annually — €112 a month, or €1,344 a year. It adds one lead-generation add-on at €32.50 a month, another €390 a year, plus a €2,500 implementation with spreadsheet migration and two training sessions. First-year total: an indicative €4,234, of which licences are only €1,344 — less than a third of the bill. In year two the implementation does not repeat: €1,734 a year for the same team.

For 20 users the same arithmetic gives these first-year totals, with the €1,000–5,000 setup band. Pipedrive Lite: 20 × €14 × 12 = €3,360, plus the €390 add-on, so €4,750–8,750. Pipedrive Growth: 20 × €39 × 12 = €9,360, plus the add-on, so €10,750–14,750. Bitrix24 Standard, which includes 50 users: €99 × 12 = €1,188, no add-on, so €2,188–6,188. None of these includes migration from another CRM (add €500–2,000) or VAT.

CRM migration cost

Moving data into the new CRM costs an indicative €500–2,000 when it comes from another CRM or from several sources; from one clean spreadsheet it usually sits inside the setup. What moves the price: the number of sources, the amount of duplicates, custom fields to map, and whether activity history and attachments must come across. Budget for overlap too: both CRMs are billed during the switchover month or months. Migration is also the cheapest moment to purge the base — contacts with no activity in the last 2–3 years inflate your costs and your reports for nothing.

Where the bill jumps

Zoho CRM's free edition stops at three users. On Bitrix24 Basic, the sixth user does not cost one more seat but the move from €49 to €99 per month. A per-seat model has no jump, only a straight line: double the team and the licences double, from €1,344 to €2,688 a year for 8 versus 16 users. The other jumps are project-sized: a second pipeline roughly doubles the configuration, because it has its own stages, fields, permissions and reports; a second integration is the most expensive step; and data from several sources, full of duplicates, adds €500–2,000 of migration.

How to compare two CRM implementation quotes

First the unit: per user, per organization or one-off licence — compare only after you state your number of users; at fifty people a €14 per-seat plan is €700 a month, while Bitrix24 Standard includes fifty users at €99. Second the billing regime: the displayed figure is usually the annual-commitment one — Pipedrive's toggle reads Billed annually (Save up to 42%). Third currency and VAT: Pipedrive states its prices are VAT exclusive, and Zarina CRM writes plus VAT next to the figure.

Fourth, what is excluded: add-ons, configuration, migration, training, the invoicing connector. Fifth, what stays yours at exit — the data export and, for a custom system, ownership of the code. Because we build software and do not resell licences, we have no stake in which subscription you pick: under 10 users with a standard sales process, a subscription is the right choice, and a custom CRM rarely pays off.

The real cost: adoption, not the subscription

From our own projects, CRM implementations fail humanly rather than technically — salespeople keep working from notebooks and spreadsheets, and the CRM becomes a graveyard of data entered under duress on Friday afternoons. A correct budget therefore includes role-based training from the start, clean contact migration (duplicates and five-year-old records are not worth importing), and integration with the tools already in use: when emails and calls log themselves automatically, the team loses its reason to bypass the system.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a CRM implementation cost?

An indicative €1,000–5,000 for a SaaS CRM configured on your process, on top of licences. A custom CRM costs €5,000–8,000 for the core and €8,000–40,000 for a full system. Migration from another CRM adds €500–2,000, and integrations are priced separately. All figures are in euros and exclude VAT.

What does a CRM implementation cost for a 20-person team?

An indicative €4,750–8,750 in year one on an entry per-seat plan. That is 20 users × €14 × 12 = €3,360 in licences, a €390 add-on and €1,000–5,000 of setup. On Pipedrive Growth, at €39 per seat, the first year is €10,750–14,750. Migration and VAT are extra.

Why does a CRM cost more to implement than to license in year one?

Because setup is one-off work and licences are a small recurring line. For 8 users on a €14 entry plan, licences are €1,344 of a €4,234 first year; a €390 add-on and €2,500 of configuration, migration and training make up the rest. In year two the implementation does not repeat, and the bill falls to €1,734.

Is starting on a free CRM plan worthwhile?

For very small teams yes, but read the threshold on the vendor's own page: the free Zoho CRM edition is advertised for three users in the title of its pricing page. Bitrix24 shows its free plan for one to two users, while its FAQ says users can be added without limit. Pipedrive offers a 14-day trial with no card.

When does a custom CRM beat a SaaS one?

After two conditions are met: you have tried 2–3 existing CRMs and your process genuinely does not fit without compromises that cost you sales, and your volume makes the licence savings over 4–5 years matter. Under 10 users with a standard sales process, the custom route rarely justifies itself.

What does migrating data into the new CRM cost?

From a clean spreadsheet, migration is often included in the implementation. From another CRM, or from several duplicate-ridden sources, budget a separate €500–2,000, indicatively. Both CRMs are billed during the switchover, so plan one or two months of overlap, and purge contacts inactive for 2–3 years first.

Does a CRM integrate with invoicing and the ERP?

Yes, and the price depends on the starting point. An e-Factura module in your existing invoicing software or ERP costs an indicative €0–500; a custom ANAF API integration €1,000–5,000. A single API link where no connector exists runs €500–3,000, while a two-way CRM–invoicing integration is €3,000–15,000.
