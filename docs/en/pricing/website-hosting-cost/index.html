Pricing guide

How much website hosting costs, across three tiers

Three hosting tiers, with the renewal price set next to the promotional one — provider prices checked on 18 November 2026.

7
sections
6
questions answered

Website hosting costs, in 2026 and indicatively before VAT, 100–400 lei a year on shared hosting for a brochure site, €20–80 a month on a VPS for a store with medium traffic, and €50–300 a month on managed cloud for an application. Checked on 18 November 2026: Hostico Start costs €1.99 a month on the first invoice and €2.99 a month at renewal (one domain, 10 GB), while ROMARG EntryPlus costs €2.95 a month (three sites, 10 GB, daily backup), with a note on its site that prices exclude VAT. The price rises not with the number of pages but with traffic and with what an hour of the site not responding costs you.

Next to hosting come a few small fixed costs: a .ro domain, 40–70 lei a year, detailed in the domain guide; the SSL certificate, €0, free through Let's Encrypt; a CDN, €0 on Cloudflare's free plan. For a brochure site, an SSL certificate invoiced separately at €50–100 a year tells you something about the rest of the offer. A VPS price does not, however, include someone to update the server: that work is maintenance, with its own price guide (€50–150 a month for a brochure site).

Key takeaways

✓Shared hosting: 100–400 lei a year

Resources shared with other sites on the same server. Sufficient for brochure sites and blogs with low traffic.

✓VPS: €20–80 a month

Guaranteed resources and full control. The reasonable tier for online stores and medium-traffic sites; the price does not include server administration, which requires technical knowledge.

✓Managed cloud: €50–300 a month

Automatic scaling at traffic peaks, managed backups, monitoring. For applications and platforms where an hour of downtime costs real money.

✓Extras: CDN and external backup

A CDN from €0 (Cloudflare has a free plan), external backup from a few euros a month. Cheap compared with what losing data or running a slow site costs you.

What you get at each tier and what is not included

On shared hosting you get a control panel (usually cPanel), an SSL certificate, backup and email, on a server shared with other sites. The limits are on processor, memory and the number of simultaneous processes. Changes to the site are not in the price: hosting keeps the site online, it does not change it. As the provider's own benchmark, ROMARG estimates roughly 30,000 visitors a month for EntryPlus and roughly 150,000 for StartPlus.

On a VPS the resources are guaranteed to you, but system and security updates stay yours unless the plan explicitly says “managed”. On managed cloud you pay by consumption, with monitoring included, so the bill moves with traffic.

The promotional price and the renewal price

The figures that show up in searches are usually the price of the first invoice. Hostico says on its hosting page that the discount applies “directly on the first invoice, regardless of the chosen period”: the Start package goes from €1.99 to €2.99 a month at renewal, and Business, the package for stores, from €4.99 to €7.99 a month.

So compare offers at the renewal price, not the promotional one, and note in your calendar the date the initial period ends. The difference matters most if you keep the site for years.

Signals that you are paying too much — or too little

You are paying too much when: your brochure site sits on a package of tens of euros a month sized for traffic you do not have; you are billed separately for SSL (free through Let's Encrypt for years now), for undefined server optimisation, or for huge storage you never use. The simple check: ask the provider for the monthly traffic and load statistics — if the server sits below 10% utilisation consistently, there is room to step down a tier.

You are paying too little when the symptoms show up on the customer's side: a slow site at peak hours, checkout errors during campaigns, emails landing in spam because the shared IP address is on blocklists. For a store selling €10,000 a month, the gap between shared hosting at 300 lei a year and a VPS at €40 a month is insignificant next to the orders lost in a single peak-season day with the site down. The practical rule: hosting cost should be invisible relative to what the site produces.

When to move from shared to a VPS or cloud

It is worth moving up a tier when orders fail or the site slows at traffic peaks after it has already been optimised, when the panel shows you have hit processor or memory limits, when you need software the shared plan does not allow, or when you add a second site or brand with traffic of its own.

Do not move up just because the package looks small: if the server sits constantly below 10% utilisation, a bigger tier brings nothing.

How to read a hosting offer: checks before you pay

Check the renewal price and whether the displayed price includes VAT: ROMARG states on its site that prices exclude VAT and that payment is made in lei; with other providers, ask. Find out where the backup is kept and how many restore points exist; ROMARG, for example, keeps 7 daily, 4 weekly and 3 monthly copies.

Also ask what happens when resources are exceeded, who applies server updates and whose name the account is in. An account in your company's name takes a minute to check and spares you trouble if you ever change agencies.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest hosting and what do you give up at that price?

On Hostico Start and ROMARG EntryPlus, checked on 18 November 2026, entry level is around €2–3 a month (Hostico Start: €1.99 on the first invoice, €2.99 on renewal). At that price you get a single domain or a few sites, limited processor and memory, and a shared server. It is enough for a brochure site, not for a store running campaigns.

Do the prices hosting companies display include VAT?

Not always. ROMARG states on its site that prices exclude VAT and that payment is made in lei. Check the total in the cart before ordering. The figures on this page are before VAT.

Should the agency hold the hosting account, or should my company?

The hosting account is best held by your company, with access granted to the agency — not the other way round. If the relationship with the agency ends badly, an account owned by them means your site and your email are under somebody else's control. The agency can happily administer it; owning the account is a different matter.

What happens if I exceed the resources in my hosting package?

It depends on the provider and it is written in the terms: some throttle the site, some suspend it temporarily, and cloud-model providers bill the extra consumption automatically. Before big marketing campaigns, notify the provider or upgrade temporarily — a planned upgrade costs a few euros, an outage at peak campaign costs the orders.

Is local hosting worth it, or should I pick an international provider?

For a Romanian audience, servers in Romania or Central Europe give very good response times, and support in your own language and time zone is a real practical advantage. On a server in Romania or another EU country, the site files stay inside the European Union; added services (CDN, email filters) can move data outside it, so check them separately. International providers win on ecosystem and scaling for complex applications. For an ordinary company website, the provider's support and seriousness matter more than the performance difference.

Is the backup included with hosting enough?

Sometimes yes, but check two things: where the copy is kept and how many restore points the provider retains. ROMARG keeps 7 daily, 4 weekly and 3 monthly copies, and Hostico lists daily and remote backup. For a store or an application, add an automated external backup with another provider: it costs a few euros a month.
