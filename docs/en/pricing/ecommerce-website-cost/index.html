Pricing guide

What an online store costs, and what you pay every month after launch

Four ways to build, each with its own monthly bill — and year one set against the price in the quote.

7
sections
6
questions answered

An online store costs in 2026, indicatively and excluding VAT, €500–3,000 to set up a Shopify store plus a €30–300/month subscription, €1,500–8,000 for a WooCommerce or PrestaShop implementation with Romanian integrations, from €8,000 on Magento for large catalogues and B2B, and from €10,000 for a custom store built around your own flows. The price moves with the number of products and attributes, the import from the old system, integrations (courier, invoicing, e-Factura, ERP), B2B and selling in several countries.

After launch come the running costs: the payment processor keeps 1–2% of each transaction (1–2.9% with the fixed fee included), premium plugins cost €100–300 a year, hosting a WooCommerce or PrestaShop store with average traffic on a VPS is €20–80/month (Shopify includes it), and maintenance is €150–500/month. The practical rule: budget the entire first year, not just the launch.

Key takeaways

✓Shopify: €30–300/month + €500–3,000 setup

Fast start, hosting and security included. The subscription runs for the life of the store, and deep customisation is limited by the platform.

✓WooCommerce / PrestaShop: €1,500–8,000

Open-source on your own hosting: full control and no mandatory subscription — but updates, security and plugin compatibility become your responsibility.

✓Magento (Adobe Commerce): from €8,000

For large catalogues, B2B selling and ERP integration. It needs a stronger server and specialised developers, so running costs are higher too.

✓Custom store: from €10,000

Justified when your flows do not fit a standard platform: per-customer pricing, product configurators, tight links to the ERP and warehouse.

✓Operations: 1–2% per transaction + maintenance

Payment processing, courier contracts, e-Factura invoicing, monthly upkeep — recurring costs that at higher volumes outgrow the initial build.

What each option includes and what it does not

On Shopify, hosting, the SSL certificate and platform updates are included in the subscription; App Store apps are separate subscriptions and premium themes are paid once. WooCommerce and PrestaShop are free software: you pay for implementation, hosting, premium plugins and updates.

Magento makes sense for a large catalogue, B2B and several stores in one, but the infrastructure and the people who maintain it cost more. A custom store covers your own flows: per-customer pricing, configurators, a tight link to the ERP. No option includes product photos and copy, or the advertising budget; neither is part of any build quote.

Running costs, by card and by cash on delivery

When customers pay by card, the processor (Stripe, Netopia or Shopify Payments) keeps roughly 1–2% of each order (1–2.9% with the fixed fee included): on €100,000 of annual card sales, that is €1,000–2,000 in fees that appear in no implementation quote.

With cash on delivery there is no processor, but there is the cash-on-delivery fee in the courier contract and the cost of refused parcels; ask for both in writing before you choose a courier. Courier integration (automatic waybills, delivery statuses) is built once but breaks often, because courier APIs keep changing. And automated invoicing through e-Factura is mandatory in both B2B and B2C relationships in Romania, so invoicing software and its connector belong in the budget from day one.

Shopify: when the platform fee appears

Checked on 18 November 2026 on shopify.com and in Shopify Help: if you collect payments through Shopify Payments (available to merchants in Romania), Shopify adds no transaction fee and you pay only the card rate, which exists on any platform. If you use an external processor, Shopify adds 2% on the Basic plan, 1% on Grow and 0.6% on Advanced; manual payments such as cash on delivery are not charged.

Example: €100,000 collected by card through an external processor, on the Basic plan, means €2,000 a year on top of the processor's own fee. Plan prices are in the Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison.

Year one, set against the implementation quote

An accessories store with 300 products on WooCommerce: €4,000 implementation, adapted theme, courier and invoicing integration included. Year one then adds, indicatively, VPS hosting at €350, maintenance at €150/month (€1,800 a year) and payment fees of €600 on €40,000 of sales. Real first-year total: about €6,750, nearly 70% above the quoted price.

That is not a trap; it is the normal cost structure of e-commerce, but you need to know it before you sign. Migrating between platforms later costs €1,000–3,000 for an average store, which is why the choice matters at the start.

When it is not worth paying an agency

Under about 50 standard products, with no special invoicing requirements, when you only want to test the market, a Shopify or a Romanian SaaS platform (Gomag, MerchantPro) set up by yourself is enough. SaaS subscriptions usually grow with order volume, so check the thresholds before you commit to one.

An agency or a custom store pays off when you have a large catalogue with variants, B2B pricing, ERP or inventory sync, and e-Factura without manual work.

Frequently asked questions

Which option fits a catalogue under 100 products on a small budget?

Shopify, with a €500–3,000 setup plus the subscription, or WooCommerce from €1,500 on a good template. You enter the market and learn within a few months whether the products sell. A custom store at zero volume is money locked into features nobody uses yet.

At what sales volume does the Shopify subscription become a problem?

Rarely the subscription; the platform fee is what grows. Shopify charges 2%, 1% or 0.6% (Basic, Grow, Advanced) only when you use an external processor, and not on cash on delivery. With Shopify Payments you pay only the card rate and no Shopify fee. Checked on 18 November 2026.

Is e-invoicing integration normally included in the price?

Rarely in base quotes, so verify it. A module in your existing invoicing software (SmartBill, Oblio, FGO) costs €0–500; a custom integration with the ANAF API €1,000–5,000; recurring, €20–100/month. Ask the quote to name the exact option.

How does store maintenance compare with a normal website?

It is noticeably higher: an indicative €150–500/month versus €50–150 for a brochure site. The reason is risk — a store that is down loses orders by the hour, and security patches on a platform that processes payments cannot wait.

Do the prices include VAT, and how is an online store paid for?

The prices on this page exclude VAT; a VAT-registered supplier adds 21% (the standard rate since 1 August 2025). Payment is usually a 20–30% advance, with the rest in delivered stages.

How long does an online store take to build?

Indicatively 4–8 weeks for a turnkey store. Importing a large catalogue and integrations with the ERP, couriers or invoicing move the date. The platform account, the domain and the code should be in your company's name.
