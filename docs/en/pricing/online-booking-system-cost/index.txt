A clinic, a salon, a car service or a guesthouse loses the customer who cannot book at 10 pm — that customer calls the competition in the morning. Route one: a plugin or booking SaaS (Calendly, SimplyBook.me and their peers) — live within days, but you pay forever and play by the platform's rules. Route two: a booking system built into your own website, where the calendar, the services and the notifications belong to you and look like your brand. Route three: a custom platform, for upfront payments, multiple resources (treatment rooms, machines, guest rooms) and staff with individual schedules. Hovering over all of it is the commission question: the listing platforms that bring you clients keep a percentage of every booking — and at decent volume, that percentage alone finances a system of your own. Wherever in Europe you operate, note that the build figures here come from the Romanian development market, which is precisely what keeps the custom routes within reach of small operators. Direct bookings avoid the Booking commission with a SaaS engine too, so volume does not decide between SaaS and your own system. Your own system is justified when the SaaS's per-booking commission, its caps or the workflows it cannot model cost more than development plus maintenance. The honest strategy is not 'leave the platforms' — they remain your acquisition channel — but 'stop letting loyal clients cost you a commission on every return'.
