Pricing guide

How much a logo and visual identity cost, on three levels

The price of a logo depends on what you get around the drawing: files, written rights and usage rules.

7
sections
6
questions answered

A logo costs in 2026, indicatively and excluding VAT, €0–100 from an AI generator or a design contest, €150–800 from a freelancer who delivers vector files, and €1,000–5,000 at a branding studio, where the price covers the full visual identity: logo variants, palette, typography and a brand manual. The price rises with the number of concepts and revision rounds, the research, the brand manual, the number of materials the identity is applied to, and the written assignment of rights. Separately from design, registering a trademark at the Romanian office OSIM costs, under the fees in force since 01.01.2026, 1,168 lei for a black-and-white figurative mark and 1,930 lei for a colour one, for one class, with certificate.

The difference between a generator and a studio is not the image but what arrives alongside it: a uniqueness check (so you do not discover two years later that an almost identical mark is already registered to somebody else), vector files in every format, variants for light and dark backgrounds, and a brand manual that keeps the identity coherent across the site, packaging and social media. Between the extremes sits the freelancer, often the best value for an SME. Checked on 18 November 2026 against the OSIM fee schedule and the OSIM SME Fund page.

Key takeaways

✓AI generator or design contest: €0–100

Fast and cheap. What you do not get: a uniqueness check, clear usage rights and, often, a vector file.

✓Freelancer: €150–800

Original design, a few proposals and revision rounds, final vector files. As a rule you do not get a trademark search or a brand manual.

✓Branding studio: €1,000–5,000

Research, a logo with variants, palette, typography and a complete manual, the base every future supplier works from consistently. It does not include OSIM fees, printing or applying the identity to the site.

What each level includes and what it does not

At the €0–100 level you receive an image: often a generic symbol that other companies use in minor variations, sometimes in bitmap formats only, unusable for large-format print. What you do not get: a uniqueness check, a clear assignment of rights, a vector file and variants for light and dark backgrounds.

From a freelancer you receive design made for you, with vector files (SVG, EPS, PDF) and revisions. What you do not get unless you ask for it in the offer: a prior-rights trademark search, usage rules, the source file and a written assignment of rights.

From a studio you receive a system: primary and secondary logo, grid construction, minimum clear space, colours with exact codes, fonts and application examples, documented in the brand manual. What you do not get: trademark fees, printing and implementation on the site or packaging outside the agreed list.

A common scenario: a company starts with a cheap logo, and when it wants to enter retail it finds that a similar mark is already registered in the same class. The logo changes, and with it the labels, packaging, site and already printed materials. A search of the OSIM database or TMview before paying costs time, not money.

Costs that remain after delivery

The trademark is registered separately, at OSIM. The 2026 fees for a figurative mark in one class: 1,168 lei black-and-white (51 lei filing, 152 publication, 559 examination, 152 figurative element, 254 certificate) and 1,930 lei colour (51 lei filing, 508 publication, 965 examination, 152 figurative element, 254 certificate). Each additional class costs 254 lei.

Renewal costs 610 lei black-and-white or 1,016 lei colour for one class, plus 152 lei for the figurative element and 254 lei for the renewal certificate.

Applying the identity to the site is separate work: design only costs roughly €100–400 per page, excluding the build in code, which is paid separately. The price of a logo therefore includes neither the site nor printing.

Help with the trademark fee: the 2026 SME Fund voucher

Voucher 2 of the EUIPO SME Fund programme (trademarks, designs and models) reopened on 05.11.2026, after the OSIM announcement: up to €700 for SMEs based in the European Union, reimbursed within the voucher limit. Applications are submitted to EUIPO by 4 December 2026.

The call closed once before, on 23.04.2026, ahead of the reopening, so support is granted within available funds. Check the OSIM SME Fund page before paying the fees, because the reimbursement rules matter.

How to read a logo offer: checks before you sign

Ask for the file list: vector (SVG, EPS or PDF), PNG on a transparent background, variants for light and dark backgrounds, HEX, RGB and CMYK colour codes, and the fonts with their licences.

Ask for the assignment in writing. Law 8/1996, art. 42(1), requires the contract to state the rights transferred and, for each, the modes of use, duration, extent and remuneration. Under art. 43, the existence and content of an assignment can be proven only in writing. If you want exclusivity, write it in expressly.

Search for the mark in the OSIM database and in TMview before paying, not after printing the packaging.

When a studio is not worth it, and what to choose instead

For testing an idea or the first months of trading, a freelancer with a written assignment and vector files is enough. If you already have a logo but every supplier uses it differently, you need only a short usage guide, not a rebrand.

If you start with a website, a basic identity can come in the digital start-up package, which includes a logo and essential materials. A studio is justified when several suppliers work with the brand at once and you want a written referee.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest option and what do you lose?

An AI generator or a design contest, at €0–100. You lose the uniqueness check, a clear assignment of rights and, often, the vector file. For testing an idea it is enough; for a brand you promote for years, the gaps end up costing money.

Which files should I receive when a logo is delivered?

At minimum: the vector version (SVG, EPS or editable PDF) — the only one that scales from favicon to building signage without loss — plus PNG exports on a transparent background, the light and dark background variants and the exact colour codes. If all you get is a JPG, you have not received a logo, only a photograph of one.

Is my logo automatically protected once I pay for it?

No. Copyright passes through a written assignment (Law 8/1996, art. 42–43), while the sign is protected as a trademark by registration at OSIM: in 2026, 1,168 lei black-and-white or 1,930 lei colour for one class, or at EUIPO for the European Union. Without registration, somebody else can register a similar mark before you.

Can I use an AI-generated logo for my company?

You can, but check two things. The US Copyright Office showed in its 29 January 2025 report that a prompt alone does not make you the author; protection appears only where a human determined the expressive elements, for example by redrawing. Exclusivity then comes from trademark registration, and the generator's terms may limit use as a trademark: read them first.

Do logo prices include VAT, and how is payment made?

No: logo and visual identity prices (€0–100 generator, €150–800 freelancer, €1,000–5,000 studio) are indicative and exclude VAT. A deposit of 20–30% is usual, with the rest due on delivery of the final files and the assignment of rights. OSIM fees are paid separately, directly to the office, and are not part of the design price.

Do I need only a logo or a complete visual identity?

It depends on how many people and suppliers touch the brand. If you alone post on Facebook, a logo with two colours is enough. When you work with a marketing agency, a print shop and a web developer at once, a complete identity with a manual stops each supplier interpreting the brand differently, which is where the studio price comes from.
