Floriculture and medicinal plants: which AFIR interventions are published today — and why the DR-18 code is not among them

Checked on 30 July 2026: the DR-18 intervention page does not exist on afir.ro, and the agency's agriculture funding sections list no intervention dedicated to floriculture or medicinal plants.

The DR-18 code does not appear in the intervention catalogue AFIR publishes. Checked on 30 July 2026, the details-and-annexes address for DR-18 returns a 404, while the agency's agriculture funding sections list DR 15 (fruit holdings), DR 16 (vegetables and potatoes), DR 19 (non-productive investments at farm level), DR 20 (livestock), DR 22 and DR 23 (processing), DR 26 (setting up irrigation systems), DR 30 (young farmer setup), DR 33 (producer groups) and sub-measure sM 4.1 — not one intervention carrying floriculture, medicinal or aromatic plants in its title. We put that in the first line because a grower who plans an investment around a code that has no published guide loses a whole session and a planting year. Below: what is actually published and with what amounts, where a flower or aromatic-plant holding has its eligibility confirmed, and which software component makes sense in a farm dossier — direct online selling, business-to-business order management, batch traceability. Northdan Soft is the software supplier on the project, not a funding consultant: we issue no eligibility verdicts and we do not draft the funding application.

What we checked on afir.ro, on 30 July 2026

Three checks, all on the agency's own site. First: the details-and-annexes address for DR-18 returns a 404, unlike those for DR 15, DR 16, DR 19, DR 20, DR 22, DR 23, DR 26, DR 30 and DR 33, which open normally. Second: the agriculture funding pages — farm holdings, agricultural processing, fruit growing, producer groups — each enumerate the interventions in their scope, and no DR-18 appears among them. Third: the submission-sessions page showed, on the day of the check, a single launch announcement, the one for the Energy scheme.

What that means and what it does not. It means a DR-18 code cannot be used today as a planning reference: it has no published guide, no annexes and no session. It does not mean the intervention was cancelled or that it cannot appear in a later version of the Strategic Plan — the absence of a page is not a decision. The correct wording for an investment memo is the one above, not either of the two certainties.

Why it matters in this sector: floriculture, medicinal and aromatic plants appear often in programme lists recompiled year after year, where codes migrate from one document version to the next without being reconfirmed at source. The rule we apply, and recommend: an intervention code is read from the AFIR page of that intervention, not from a list.

The interventions published now, and their amounts

DR 16, investments in vegetables and potatoes: public support of at most EUR 3 million per project for associative forms in the vegetable sector, and EUR 2,000,000 per project otherwise, except projects that merely purchase machinery, capped at EUR 300,000 per project for crops other than potatoes and at EUR 700,000 where the machinery is bought by associative forms. The intensity of non-reimbursable public support does not exceed 65% of eligible costs.

DR 20, livestock: at most EUR 2 million per project, or EUR 300,000 for projects proposing only the purchase of agricultural machinery and equipment, again at an intensity of at most 65%. DR 30, young farmer setup: EUR 70,000 as a lump sum in two instalments — 75% on signing the financing contract and 25% subject to correct implementation of the business plan — for holdings between EUR 12,000 and EUR 100,000 SO, between 8,000 and 100,000 SO in mountain areas, and between 2,300 and 100,000 SO for vegetables in protected spaces.

None of these interventions carries floriculture or medicinal plants in its title, and DR 30 and sub-measure sM 4.1 (simple purchases of agricultural machinery) name no sector at all. Whether a flower or aromatic-plant holding fits one of them is decided in that intervention's applicant guide and its eligible-activity annex, not on this page. We do not issue that verdict.

The software component of a farm dossier with direct sales

Direct selling changes the economics of a floriculture holding, because the margin stays with the grower. The online store we build handles the specifics of perishable goods: a catalogue tied to seasonal availability, short delivery windows, a separate price list for florists and processors, recurring orders for trade customers. A telephone order from the partner florist and an online order land in the same ledger, so stock is deducted once.

For medicinal and aromatic plants, batch traceability is a negotiating argument: the processor or exporter asks for the plot, the harvest date, the drying and packing conditions. The system builds the batch record from data entered along the flow, not from a register filled in at month end.

In the dossier these systems appear as a technical offer broken down by functionality and as a budget by eligible cost category. One detail that counts at evaluation: AFIR publishes a reference-prices section among its working instruments, and a budget line departing from them needs justification. At implementation, the payment claim rests on the acceptance reports we hand over at delivery.

What we do, what we do not do, and where we stop

We deliver software and the technical documents describing it: specifications, offer, budget, supply contract, acceptance reports. We do not write the funding application, we do not produce the feasibility study, we do not calculate scores and we do not promise selection — those belong to the agricultural consultant and to AFIR's evaluation.

The limit that costs us something, stated before signing: we do not put into a budget a system whose classification as an eligible cost is not supported by the intervention guide, even though it would enlarge the project and our own contract. A module rejected at verification moves the whole cost onto the farm and spoils the acceptance report. We prefer a smaller budget that can actually be reimbursed.

Frequently asked questions

Does intervention DR-18 exist in PNS 2023–2027?⌄

At the check on 30 July 2026 we did not find it published: the DR-18 details-and-annexes page on afir.ro returns a 404, and the agriculture funding sections do not list it. We do not claim it was cancelled — only that it has no guide, annexes or session published today.

Which AFIR interventions are published now for farm investments?⌄

The farm-holdings section shows DR 16 (vegetables and potatoes), DR 19 (non-productive investments at farm level), DR 20 (livestock), DR 26 (setting up irrigation systems), DR 30 (young farmer setup) and sub-measure sM 4.1. Fruit growing has DR 15; processing has DR 22 and DR 23.

Is there a line dedicated to floriculture or medicinal plants?⌄

We did not identify one in the published catalogue. The interventions that name a sector cover fruit growing, vegetables and potatoes, and livestock. Whether a flower or aromatic-plant crop qualifies is read in the guide and annexes of the intervention you intend to apply to.

When do the sessions open and where do I find out for certain?⌄

From the submission-sessions page on afir.ro, where the agency publishes its launch announcements. On 30 July 2026 a single launch announcement was listed there, the one for the Energy scheme. Calendars republished on other sites stay indicative until the agency's own announcement.

Which part of the dossier does Northdan Soft cover for a flower or plant grower?⌄

The supplier's part: system specifications, the technical offer by functionality, the budget by eligible cost category, implementation and the acceptance reports for payment claims. We do not draft the funding application, produce the feasibility study or establish the holding's eligibility.
