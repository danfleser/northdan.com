As of our check on 18 November 2026, the Microindustrializare programme has no session announced for 2026: MEDAT has published no new procedure, enrolment calendar or public consultation, and the last session was the 2023 one. On the ministry page the most recent programme-related item remains the 10 November 2025 announcement on simplifying implementation for beneficiaries of already contracted sessions. So there is today no amount per project, no eligible-cost list and no opening date for 2026 that can be quoted as a rule. The last conditions applied are the 2023 ones: a grant of at most 50% of eligible costs, but no more than RON 500,000 per beneficiary, granted only together with an investment loan of at least RON 250,000 from a partner bank (procedure OM 598/2023, point 5.4). For a manufacturer planning machinery and the software that coordinates it, the useful question is not how much is on offer but what can be written down before a session appears. Northdan Soft supplies the software component — production-flow analysis, the functional specification, the working budget by cost category. We are not a funding consultant, we do not assess a company’s eligibility and we do not file applications.

What was checked, when, and with what result

At our check on 18 November 2026 the ministry page carried no applicant guide for the 2026 edition and no announced session. The most recent programme-related item dates from 10 November 2025 and concerns implementation, not an opening: through Order no. 2,225/03.11.2025, published in Monitorul Oficial no. 1034 of 7 November 2025, the Microindustrializare procedure approved by ministerial order 598/2023 was amended so that beneficiaries may repay their contracted loans partially or early without affecting receipt of the non-repayable aid, with early repayment of the loan component tied to that aid possible after the settlement file is filed.

The procedure the ministry was amending in November 2025 is still the 2023 session’s. It provides (point 2.1) that the scheme is valid until 31 December 2027, with payments until 31 December 2028, so a new session can open either on this procedure, amended by order, or on a new one; either way the act appears in Monitorul Oficial, and at our check there was none. This is our reading, not a ministry statement. The procedure estimated RON 85 million and 170 beneficiaries per year for 2024–2027 (point 2.2), yet no session followed 2023.

The only recent move is public, not official: on 2 November 2026, at Banking Forum 2026, the president of IMM România, Florin Jianu, asked for a microindustrialisation programme to be included in the 2027 budget (Agerpres). It is a proposal, not a decision.

On a mature call, three different deadlines are read separately: the submission deadline, the implementation completion deadline and the date by which payments may still be made. For the 2026 edition none of the three exists yet. We will publish them separately once they appear in the guide, because projects are lost more often at the implementation deadline than at the submission one.

Why we publish no amount per project for 2026

The figures circulating about Microindustrializare 2026 come from two places, and neither of them is a rule. The first is the archive: ceilings and intensities from earlier editions, which changed from one session to the next. The second is the budget process: proposals for commitment appropriations discussed before a budget is adopted, which do not become an allocation until adoption and do not become a per-project ceiling even after it.

The distinction matters in practice. A manufacturer who sizes an investment on an archive figure risks rewriting the whole budget after the guide appears, and a budget rewritten in a hurry is exactly where ineligible lines show up. That is why we work on budget scenarios rather than on an assumed ceiling.

What the 2023 edition set: a grant up to RON 500,000, with a mandatory loan

The 2023 session introduced a hybrid model, because any new edition will be compared against it. The non-repayable financial allocation was at most 50% of eligible costs, but no more than RON 500,000 (point 5.4 and the procedure’s annex), so the RON 500,000 ceiling was reached at a project of about RON 1,000,000 in eligible costs. The grant was given only together with a bank investment loan of at least RON 250,000, taken out with one of the partner banks. The bank checked bankability first, then AIMM checked eligibility.

The 2023 eligibility conditions show who could have applied: an SME founded by 31 December 2019 (point 3.2 c), one company per shareholder (3.2 d), activity authorised at the trade register for at least 12 months, on a single CAEN Rev. 2 code (3.2 g), and keeping or increasing headcount for 6 months after the payment agreement (3.2 m). Selection was first come, first served, with the online form open for 5 working days, extendable (point 7 c). A new edition may use different benchmarks.

The effect on a software budget is direct: in a model with a mandatory loan, the digital component no longer competes with the machine for a grant, it enters a repayment plan. A production ERP that shortens the order cycle and gives the real cost per batch changes role — from eligible cost to bankability argument.

The route that is open right now for a manufacturer

The MEDAT line with an active 2026 submission window is SME Eco-Tech, financed from the Swiss contribution: submissions until 30 December 2026, a grant of at most 40% of eligible costs, a ceiling of CHF 50,000 and a mandatory bank loan covering at least 60% of the project. Since July 2026 the beneficiary circle is no longer limited to manufacturing, so eligibility is checked in the programme's consolidated procedure rather than against the profile described here.

It is a different programme, with a different funder, a different eligible-cost list and a different scoring grid — not a substitute for Microindustrializare and not a fit for every company profile. We say so because a firm waiting passively for an unannounced edition loses a window that closes on 30 December 2026 regardless. The digital package in the consolidated SME Eco-Tech procedure accepts software up to RON 15,000 excluding VAT, but conditions are checked in the programme’s own procedure.

The software component that sits next to the machine

In the 2023 session, software, licences, IT equipment, the website or online shop and the electronic signature formed an eligible category of their own (point 6.1 d), alongside machinery and the other categories. Each cost had to be tied to the activity flow of the financed CAEN code (point 6.2). VAT, transport and commissioning were not eligible (6.3), and assets had to be new, with no leasing or second-hand (6.4–6.5). In practice that means a production ERP with recipes, work orders and consumption, stock management for raw materials and finished goods, plus automations that pull data straight off the machines.

The common mistake is treating software as an accessory to the machine, budgeted last from whatever is left. A poorly monitored production line makes the same parts, but without a real cost per order, without batch traceability and without machine load figures — that is, without precisely the indicators any progress report asks for.

How to write the software-to-machine link and itemise the budget

The link is written down, not implied. For each machine in the investment we note what data it produces, who enters it today and on what paper, which function takes it into the system and which indicator becomes measurable: machine load, real consumption per order, time from release to delivery, scrap per batch. A function that cannot be attached to any machine or financed flow does not belong in the dossier budget; it is bought separately, from own funds, and the offer says so.

The software budget is itemised by module, not as one line “production software”: effort in hours per role at a unit rate, the machine or flow served and the matching deliverable. Integrations with existing equipment are quoted individually, because they differ a lot between a machine with a digital interface and one read by hand. Licences appear by number of users and period, shop-floor equipment by type and quantity, operator training by number of sessions, and annual maintenance separately, so it is clear what the company carries after the project. A single line with a round sum cannot be compared with other offers or accepted in part.

What we will not sign before the guide

We do not issue a dated, signed technical offer for a call whose guide is unpublished. An offer dated before the rules exist can contradict the final eligible-cost list, and a line ruled ineligible at evaluation costs far more than an offer delayed by two weeks. Until publication we work on a draft document, not a firm commercial commitment — and yes, that means we postpone a deliverable clients ask us for.

We also do not issue accommodation quotes and we do not judge your company's eligibility: the first is a practice penalised at evaluation, the second belongs to your consultant or to the authority. What we deliver is the specification, the technical offer, the budget by category, the implementation and the acceptance documents required at reimbursement.

Frequently asked questions

When does Microindustrializare 2026 open?

There is no announced date. On 18 November 2026 no new procedure or consultation had been published, and the 2023 scheme stays valid until 31 December 2027. On 2 November 2026 IMM România publicly asked for the programme in the 2027 budget, which is a proposal, not a decision.

How much funding does Microindustrializare give?

In the 2023 edition the grant was at most 50% of eligible costs, but no more than RON 500,000 per beneficiary, and was given only together with an investment loan of at least RON 250,000 from a partner bank (OM 598/2023, point 5.4). The next edition may change the amounts.

Can I finance software alone, without buying machinery?

Under the 2023 procedure, formally yes: software, licences and IT equipment were an eligible category of their own (point 6.1 d), and a project could cover one or more categories. The practical condition was the loan of at least RON 250,000 and tying each cost to the financed CAEN activity. 2026 rules may differ.

Which manufacturing programme has an open submission window in 2026?

From the MEDAT portfolio, SME Eco-Tech accepts submissions until 30 December 2026, with a grant of at most 40% and a ceiling of CHF 50,000, compulsorily paired with a bank loan. Since July 2026 it no longer addresses manufacturing alone, and its eligibility conditions and cost list are its own, distinct from Microindustrializare.

What can I concretely prepare until the guide is published?

Three things that do not depend on the rules: a map of the production flow showing where information is lost, the list of target machines with the data they can emit, and the functional specification of the system linking them. Also check the company’s founding date and the CAEN Rev. 3 reclassification. The working budget is recalculated within days of the guide.

Who could apply to Microindustrializare in 2023?

The 2023 procedure required an SME founded by 31 December 2019, with activity authorised at the trade register for at least 12 months on an eligible manufacturing CAEN Rev. 2 code, and one company per shareholder (point 3.2). Checking a company’s eligibility stays with the consultant or the authority.
