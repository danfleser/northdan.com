AFIR funding for agricultural processing: DR 22, DR 23, and what DR 30 is not

Agricultural processing is funded through AFIR under DR 22 (65%, at most EUR 3, 7 or 10 million per project) and DR 23 (at most EUR 10 million for bakery greenfield projects, EUR 3 million otherwise); on 18 November 2026 neither has an open session, and the last one, under DR 23, closed on 16 February 2026.

A processor can apply for AFIR funds under DR 22, for the conditioning, storage and processing of agricultural and fruit products, with 65% of eligible costs and at most EUR 3, 7 or 10 million per project (DR 22 guide, 2.6), or under DR 23, for products outside Annex 1 to the TFEU, with at most EUR 10 million for greenfield bakery investments and EUR 3 million otherwise (DR 23 intervention fact sheet, version 8.0). Checked on 18 November 2026 against the AFIR documents and the submission portal, which lists no open session under DR 22 or DR 23. The code DR 30, sometimes listed in place of processing, is in fact young farmer setup: a EUR 70,000 lump sum. Northdan Soft enters such projects as the supplier of the IT systems — a food production ERP, batch traceability — and of the technical documents the dossier requires, not as a funding consultancy.

DR 22: conditioning, storage and processing, with three ceilings

Beneficiaries come in two kinds (DR 22 guide, 2.1): associative forms with an economic role — agricultural cooperatives, cooperative companies, producer groups and organisations recognised by the agriculture ministry — and other economic operators: SMEs under Law no. 346/2004, large enterprises, and farmers classified as an SME or a large enterprise.

The rate of non-reimbursable public support is 65% of eligible costs, and the value does not exceed EUR 3 million per project for modernisation, EUR 10 million for greenfield projects proposing at least processing for the fruit and vegetable, milling, oil and compound-feed sectors, and EUR 7 million for other greenfield investments (DR 22 guide, 2.6). The difference between modernisation and greenfield moves the ceiling by millions, so it is settled at the start.

The last DR 22 session opened on 20 December 2023 with EUR 210.3 million and was listed to close on 30 April 2024 (call page, oportunitati-ue.gov.ro). AFIR closed the session early, in February 2024, because the funds ran out, and no DR 22 session has opened since. The 2025 budget top-up (about EUR 249 million) paid for projects waitlisted from 2023 and opened no new session.

DR 23: processing towards products outside Annex 1 to the TFEU

DR 23 targets processing of agricultural products to obtain food products and transformed products other than those listed in Annex 1 to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Beneficiaries: SMEs under Law no. 346/2004 and large enterprises; the guide (2.1) lists the eligible legal forms: authorised sole traders (PFA), individual or family enterprises, SRL, SA, SNC, SCS, SCA, agricultural cooperatives, cooperative societies and producer groups.

The ceiling is at most EUR 10 million per project for greenfield bakery investments (NACE 1071, 1072, 1073 and 1085, per the guide footnote, 2.6) and EUR 3 million otherwise. The rate is at most 65% (DR 23 guide, 2.6), but capped by the Regional Aid Map 2022–2027 (Annexes 15.1 and 15.2): a flat 65% does not apply everywhere.

The map also decides where you can apply: Bucharest is not in the list of eligible zones, only 28 localities of Ilfov are eligible, and there a large enterprise qualifies only for a new economic activity. The work points of one processing flow must be in the same county (DR 23 guide, 2.2). Document source: the intervention fact sheet, version 8.0 of 28 November 2025.

The two interventions are not chosen by preference: the dividing line is the resulting product, namely whether it appears in Annex 1 to the TFEU. That check belongs to the consultant and the guide, not to the software supplier.

The 2025–2026 DR 23 session and what comes next

Announcement A1.2/01/2025, published on 28 November 2025, opened submission from 15 December 2025, 9:00, to 16 February 2026, 16:00. The allocation was EUR 164,929,100, split equally: EUR 82,464,550 for greenfield and EUR 82,464,550 for modernisation. The minimum score was 45 points, with quality thresholds of 70 points (15 December 2025 – 14 January 2026) and 45 points (15 January – 16 February 2026). Submission stopped at 150% of a component's allocation, except during the first five days of each stage.

According to the AFIR communiqué of 17 February 2026, the session closed with 273 applications and EUR 525.5 million requested, i.e. 319% of the allocation; the greenfield component was oversubscribed within the first five days. Selection reports are published on afir.ro under Reports, Selection. On 18 November 2026 the AFIR submission portal lists no new session under DR 22 or DR 23 and no date has been announced.

Digital-solution scoring: where software counts

Under DR 23, criterion CS 3.2 for greenfield gives at most 19 points: distinct digital solutions worth 5–10% of eligible value earn 12 points and over 10% earn 15; solutions integrated into the technology line worth 10–15% earn 15 points and over 15% earn 19. Scores are not cumulative; the highest counts. For modernisation, CS 5.2 gives at most 13 points: 5–10% earns 10, over 10% earns 13, and integrated 10–15% earns 10 while over 15% earns 13 (DR 23 guide, 2.5, session 01/2025). Under DR 22, CS 6.3 gives 8 points at 5% or more of eligible value (DR 22 guide, 2.5).

The condition is that the digital investment appears in a budget specific to this type of investment. Software is an eligible cost as an intangible asset only if identified as necessary in the technical-economic documentation, and under DR 23 it is bought from unrelated third parties at market conditions (guides, 2.3). The guides' examples are scales linked to the management system, raw material reception and consumption by recipe.

What share of the value goes to digital is a scoring decision for the consultant and the designer, not for us.

What DR 30 actually is, so you do not lose a session

DR 30 is support for young farmer setup: a EUR 70,000 lump sum in two instalments, 75% on signing the contract and 25% subject to correct implementation of the business plan, which runs for at most three years, or five for orchard and table-grape holdings (DR 30 fact sheet, condition 6). The beneficiary must be a micro or small enterprise (condition 3), and investments must be at least 40% of the support (condition 5). SO thresholds are EUR 12,000–100,000, 8,000–100,000 in mountain areas and 2,300–100,000 for vegetables in protected spaces.

A processor looking for money for a packing line has nothing to submit under DR 30, and a young farmer at setup has nothing to submit under DR 22. Consolidation of young farmers' holdings is another intervention, DR 12, covered separately.

The systems that run the line, and how they enter the budget

The first layer is the food production ERP: production orders based on recipes, automatic write-off of consumption, records of batches and yields. It answers the question every processor asks at month end — what each product leaving the gate actually costs, raw material, process losses and labour included.

The second layer is traceability: every batch of finished product keeps its link to the raw material batches used, to the process parameters and to the deliveries made. At a market withdrawal you isolate the affected batches in minutes instead of freezing a whole period's output.

In the dossier both layers appear as a technical offer broken down by functionality and as a budget by eligible cost category, aligned with the physical investment schedule — an ERP is not accepted before the line it runs exists. Indicatively, a bespoke management core sits in the EUR 10,000–15,000 band and a modular system started step by step in the EUR 15,000–60,000 band; the firm figure follows a review of the process flow.

Where our role stops

We deliver the systems and their documents: specifications, technical offer, budget, supply contract, acceptance reports for payment claims. We do not draft the funding application, we do not produce the feasibility study, we do not decide whether a project belongs under DR 22 or DR 23 and we do not guarantee selection — evaluation belongs exclusively to AFIR.

The limit that costs us: we do not issue an offer we cannot honour at the budgeted value. In a processing project, where software acceptance depends on the line being commissioned, an optimistic offer turns into a delayed payment claim. We would rather say up front that integration with certain machines is only possible once we have read their technical sheets, even if that shortens the feature list in the offer.

Frequently asked questions

Which intervention does an agrifood processing project go to?

To DR 22, for the conditioning, storage and processing of agricultural and fruit products, or to DR 23, where the result is a food or transformed product outside Annex 1 to the TFEU. The dividing line is the resulting product, and each intervention's annex of eligible domains confirms it.

Is the DR 23 session still open?

No. The DR 23 session ran from 15 December 2025 to 16 February 2026 with EUR 164.9 million and closed with EUR 525.5 million requested. On 18 November 2026 the AFIR submission portal lists no new session under DR 22 or DR 23.

How many points do digital solutions earn under DR 23?

Up to 19 points for greenfield (CS 3.2) and 13 for modernisation (CS 5.2), depending on their share of eligible value: 5–10% earns 12 and 10 points, over 10% earns 15 or 13; solutions integrated into the line can reach 19 points for greenfield. The highest score counts.

Can a company in Bucharest apply under DR 23?

Not for an investment made in Bucharest: the municipality is not in the list of eligible zones of the Regional Aid Map (DR 23 fact sheet, guide 2.2). In Ilfov county only 28 localities are eligible, and a large enterprise can apply there only for a new economic activity.

What does DR 30 actually finance?

Young farmer setup: a EUR 70,000 lump sum in two instalments of 75% and 25%, with a business plan implemented in at most three years (five for orchard and table grapes), for micro and small enterprises with holdings between EUR 12,000 and EUR 100,000 SO. It is not a processing intervention.

What are the ceilings under DR 22?

The rate of non-reimbursable public support is 65% of eligible costs, and the value does not exceed EUR 3 million per project for modernisation, EUR 10 million for greenfield projects including processing for the fruit and vegetable, milling, oil and compound-feed sectors, and EUR 7 million for other greenfield investments.
