The programme's official applicant guide
ⓘNorthdan Soft is a software supplier, not a funding consultancy, and is not affiliated with any managing authority or development agency. Programme information is indicative — always check the conditions in the programme's official applicant guide.

Call 2 of Action 2.2 in PR Centru 2021-2027 accepts funding applications in MySMIS2021 from 02.12.2026, 12:00, to 02.02.2027, 12:00 (applicant guide, sections 4.3.1, 4.3.2 and 4.4), under the code PRC/1059/PRC_P2/OP1/RSO1.2/PRC_A32. The final guide was published on 30.10.2026. It is a competitive call with a deadline and a budget of EUR 14,113,032 (section 3.3): a non-repayable grant between EUR 15,000 and EUR 200,000 per project, under de minimis rules, covering at most 90% of eligible value, so at least 10% own contribution (sections 3.4, 5.4 and 5.5). SMEs in Alba, Brașov, Covasna, Harghita, Mureș and Sibiu can apply if they operate outside the ICT sector, have at least one full fiscal year of activity, net or operating profit above zero in the previous fiscal year, and at least one employee. The project must include intangible assets, meaning software, and no company can apply without a digital maturity audit done before the application and a digitalization plan. This page walks through those rules in the order a company meets them, with the guide section next to each, checked on 18 November 2026 against the final guide and its annexes.

How we help

Calendar, call code and official source

Call opening: 30.10.2026 (guide, section 4.1). Submission opens 02.12.2026, 12:00, and closes 02.02.2027, 12:00 (sections 4.3.1 and 4.3.2), exclusively in MySMIS2021 (section 4.4). Call code PRC/1059/PRC_P2/OP1/RSO1.2/PRC_A32; managing authority ADR Centru. The official call page is on regiocentru.ro, linked from this page.

ADR Centru announced an online information session on 23.11.2026 at 12:00 on Zoom, with registration through a form published in the ADR Centru announcement. Questions to the authority go to helpdesk@adrcentru.ro, answered within 10 working days (guide, section 1.1).

Selection runs by month (section 8.4). Month 1 is 02.12.2026, 12:00 - 02.01.2027, 24:00: projects filed in it go to contracting with at least 85 points, if they fit within the available allocation; those above 50 and below 85 points stay in competition with month-2 projects, until 02.02.2027, if the allocation is not covered. If the call has closed and the total amount requested by all projects does not cover the allocation, every project with at least 50 points can go to contracting. Evaluation follows filing order, within 20 working days at most; clarification answers are due in 5 working days and cannot add improvements.

Filing in December or in January: what changes

For a firm with a complete file, filing in the first month, between 02.12.2026, 12:00, and 02.01.2027, 24:00, has no disadvantage at selection compared with January, and a project with at least 85 points gets priority for the allocation in it (guide, section 8.4); this is our reading of the mechanism, not a recommendation in the guide. Exception: if the 2026 figures (profit, employees, turnover for criterion 3.2) are more favourable than those for 2025, calculate the score for both years before you choose. First-month projects with at least 85 points go to contracting after the whole session is evaluated and the appeals are settled, only if they fit the allocation. Those with 50-84 points stay in competition and are ranked by score, descending, together with the month-2 projects. If the allocation runs out before the call closes, the projects that no longer fit go to the reserve list. The figure 85 is a selection condition, not an excellence threshold: at section 8.6 the guide says that one is “not applicable”.

Several conditions refer to the “fiscal year before submission”: profit above 0 lei (section 5.1.1.6), an average of at least one employee (5.1.1.7), the SME category (5.1.2) and the turnover in criterion 3.2, where an investment of at most 20% of it earns 5 points (Annex 4). For an application filed in December 2026 that year is 2025; for one filed in 2027 it is 2026. Our reading: the same for filings on 01-02.01.2027, which are still the first month for selection but already 2027 for the fiscal-year rules. A firm with a loss in 2025 and a profit in 2026 cannot file in December; in January it can file only if it already has the 2026 financial statements approved by the general meeting, showing a profit, at the date of submission (sections 5.1.1.6 and 7.4, point 2); one with a profit in 2025 and a loss in 2026 should not wait for January. The guide’s example covers only 2026 and has no 2027 equivalent.

The financial statements need a separate decision. At submission the guide asks for those approved by the general meeting for the previous financial year (section 7.4, point 2), meaning 2026 for January, and it does not say whether those for 2025 are accepted until they are approved. A firm aiming at January asks helpdesk@adrcentru.ro in the first half of December, the answer coming within 10 working days at most (section 1.1). Outside the two hour limits (02.01.2027, 24:00, and 02.02.2027, 12:00), the hour of submission matters only on a score tie, after criteria 1, 3, 2 and 4 (section 8.4). Do not rush an incomplete file: a project rejected at evaluation cannot be filed again in this call (8.7), and a missing or blank digitalization plan means rejection without clarifications (8.4).

Who can apply: eligibility conditions

Legal form: a company registered under Law 31/1990 or the Cooperatives Law 1/2005, micro, small or medium under Law 346/2004, with SME status checked at submission and at contract signing (sections 5.1 and 5.1.2). Branches and other units without legal personality are not eligible (section 5.1). The site where the investment happens must be in the Centru Region, urban or rural (section 3.5).

Track record: activity covering at least one full fiscal year (section 5.1.1.6). Profit: net or operating profit above 0 lei in the fiscal year before submission. The guide's example is written for 2026 submissions: a company established by 3 January 2025 at the latest, with profit at 31 December 2025. At submission the annual financial statements approved by the general meeting are required, for the financial year before submission (section 7.4, item 2). For January 2027 submissions the previous year is 2026, and the guide gives no example for this window and does not say what counts if the 2026 statements are not yet approved: a company planning to file in January should ask the helpdesk first.

Employees: an average of at least 1 employee in the previous fiscal year, or at least one full-time employee on an open-ended contract, held at submission and at contracting (section 5.1.1.7). A company may file one application in this call (section 5.1.1.8). Co-financing may come from own funds or external finance that involves no public support, for example a bank loan without a public guarantee (section 5.1.1.5).

The general exclusions of section 5.1.1.2 apply as in other programmes: bankruptcy, insolvency, liquidation, firm in difficulty, an unexecuted state-aid recovery order, unpaid tax obligations.

The CAEN condition: not only "non-IT"

The call's aim is to raise digitalization of SMEs active in sectors other than ICT (section 3.6). The test covers the whole company, not just the digitalized activity: at submission the applicant must hold no activated or authorised CAEN code from Annex 3 and must have had no revenue from excluded activities in the last fiscal year and the current year (section 5.1.1.1). A code is "activated" when the company books economic activity under it (footnote 6).

Annex 3 is not only information technology. Besides software publishing and development (5821, 5829, 6201, 6202, 6203, 6209), data processing and web portals (6311, 6312, 6399), telecommunications, and the manufacture, trade and repair of computers and electronics, the list of excluded codes covers: tobacco; waste collection and treatment; coal, oil, gas and the trade in fuels, including petrol stations (4671, 4730); agriculture, hunting, fishing and aquaculture; part of the food industry (meat, fish, fruit and vegetables, oils, dairy, milling, starch, sugar, spices, animal feed); alcoholic drinks and malt (1101-1106); agri-food wholesale (4611, 4617, 4621-4639); non-specialised food shops (4711) and specialised food shops (4721-4729); and gambling (9200). Not on the list, for example: bread and pastry production (1071), biscuits (1072), pasta (1073), chocolate and sweets (1082), coffee (1083), ready meals (1085), ice cream (1052) and soft drinks and mineral water (1107). Check each authorised code against the annex rows, not by the name of the sector.

In addition, the digitalized activity must have CAEN codes authorised at submission and revenue from them in the submission year and the year before (sections 5.2.1 and 5.7.4). Check the codes first, because contracting requires the ORC certificate and the trial balance with revenue by CAEN code (section 7.6).

What is funded, and the ceilings in the guide

The project must include intangible assets; without them the application is ineligible (sections 3.6 and 5.2.4). Tangible assets are optional and accepted only when they integrate into the digitalization plan; productive equipment is outside the call even if used digitally. The base activity must be at least 50% of the eligible budget (section 5.2.3).

Base activities (section 5.2.2): adopting digital technologies in existing processes (RPA, CRM, PLM, ERP, VR and AR, IoT), including temporary subscription software licences; artificial-intelligence solutions; digital communication with suppliers and customers, for example e-invoicing or a digitalized ordering circuit; the digital maturity audit; employee training. Related activities: consultancy, financial audit, information and publicity.

Ceilings and conditions by cost category (section 5.3.2). Bought or custom-built software, websites and online shops, cybersecurity: no ceiling, provided they are used exclusively by the beneficiary, bought from third parties unrelated to the buyer and kept at least 3 years after project completion. Equipment tied to digitalization, LAN and WiFi, archive digitization: no amount ceiling. Inventory items: only above 2,500 lei per unit. Cloud, SaaS, PaaS, IaaS: eligible only for the implementation period, with durability-period costs borne from own funds. Training: EUR 658.63 per employee who completed a programme of at least 12 hours, with a certificate. Consultancy (application, management, digitalization plan): up to 5% of base-activity spending. Information and publicity: up to 7,500 lei excluding VAT. Financial audit, optional: up to 5,000 lei excluding VAT per quarter. Digital maturity audit: the guide sets no ceiling.

Ineligible (section 5.3.3): land, second-hand items, running and maintenance costs, personnel costs outside the training unit cost, leasing, vehicles, spending before 01.01.2021 and costs of starting the investment before submission, meaning the first binding order (glossary, item 11). Exception: application consultancy, the digitalization plan and the initial audit can be bought before submission. VAT is eligible only when it is not recovered. Amounts above the guide's ceilings stay with the company.

De minimis: aid is granted under Regulation (EU) 2831/2023, with a EUR 300,000 ceiling for a single undertaking over any rolling 3 years (section 3.13). A EUR 200,000 project is not automatically available to a company: what the single undertaking, including linked firms, has already received is deducted from the ceiling.

The digital maturity audit: the independence rule

The guide requires two audits, each with a report: one before the application is filed and one at the end of implementation (section 5.3.2.11). The initial report measures the digitalization level against the 12 DESI 2019 criteria and identifies the technical solutions needed, provided they become investments in the project. It is a mandatory annex at submission (section 7.4, item 6) and supports sub-criterion 1.5 in the grid. The final report is filed with the last payment request and certifies progress and the maturity reached. Report templates are in Annex 13.

Who may audit (section 5.3.2.11): an independent external auditor, distinct from the beneficiary and not subordinated to it or to the suppliers of the services and products it audits, a legal entity authorised for CAEN 6202 (Rev.2) or 6220 (Rev.3), or a European Digital Innovation Hub, EDIH. Section 5.3.2.8 adds that the consultancy writing the digitalization plan cannot supply the IT solution or draft the audit report in the same project. Consequence: software supplier, auditor and plan author are three different roles on one project.

The initial audit cost is eligible even when incurred before submission, if not before 01.01.2021 (section 5.3.1). If the final report does not show indicator RCR 13 met, that is at least 7 of 12 DESI criteria, the company must file in the durability period, within one year of completion at most, a post-implementation report proving it (section 3.8.2).

The digitalization plan, Annex 6

The digitalization plan is an annex to the funding application and must contain a description of current activity and of the project (section 5.3.2.8). A missing plan, a missing financial template or either one attached uncompleted means rejection, with no clarifications (sections 7.4 and 8.4). The Annex 7 template is also e-mailed in Excel to secretariat_prc@adrcentru.ro after submission.

Structure in Annex 6: general company data; description of current activity, with 3-year indicators and the existing IT infrastructure as found in the initial audit; description of the project, with the processes digitalized, the solution architecture, the implementation plan, risks, training and a cost table in which every line shows the unit price excluding VAT and the DESI 2019 criterion it meets; the financial projections in the Annex 7 template; conclusions. The guide does not restrict who writes the plan: the company's own team or a consultant. If a consultant writes it, they cannot be the solution supplier or the auditor in the same project, and the cost counts against the 5% consultancy ceiling.

The scoring grid in brief (Annex 4)

The grid has 100 points and the quality threshold is 50 (sections 3.1 and 8.5). The four chapters: contribution to the objective, 55 points; quality, maturity and sustainability, 21; financial and operational capacity, 17; horizontal principles and DNSH, 7. Four sub-criteria are eliminatory, meaning 0 points on them means rejection: DESI 2019 progress (1.1), quality of the digitalization plan (2.3), cumulative net cash flow negative at any time (2.4) and DNSH (4.2).

The heaviest sub-criteria: reaching 7 of 12 DESI criteria plus at least one of ERP, CRM, IoT, cloud services, AI, VR/AR or RPA is worth 15 points (1.2); training at least 3 employees, 15 points (1.3); over 10 unique annual users of the solutions, 10 points (1.4); collaboration with an EDIH in at least one activity, up to 4 points, depending on how well the application describes it (2.5). Own contribution to eligible value (3.1): 10%-15% inclusive, 1 point; over 15% to 20% inclusive, 4 points; over 20% to 25% inclusive, 8 points; over 25%, 12 points. So exactly 20% earns 4 points, 3 more than the 10% minimum; the next band starts above 20%. The guide recommends no 20% figure. The grid is explained criterion by criterion, with all bands, on the dedicated scoring page.

Tie-break: criteria 1, 3, 2 and 4, in that order, then the date and time of submission (section 8.4). The grid does not score new jobs, county, company size or RIS3 domain.

Payment, deadlines and durability

Payment mechanisms are pre-financing, payment request and reimbursement request (section 12). The model contract (Annex 15) provides pre-financing in tranches of at most 10% of the contract's eligible value, and payment and reimbursement requests by private beneficiaries have a minimum value of 100,000 lei, below which the authority returns them. At the guide's rate of 5.2786 lei per euro a EUR 15,000 grant is about 79,179 lei, a calculation of ours: the documents do not say how the threshold applies to a project that small, so the question goes to the helpdesk. Authority verification takes 15 working days for pre-financing and 20 for payment or reimbursement, plus at most 10 for clarifications. The final reimbursement request is filed within 60 calendar days of the end of implementation, with a final on-site visit.

Annex 14 sets milestones: claims for 25% of the base activity within 12 months at most and for 75% within 20 months, and, under Annex 15, procurement files are uploaded in MySMIS within 10 working days of signing each contract. Implementation lasts at most 24 months (section 5.6) and must start within 6 months of the contract, or the authority may terminate (Annex 15). An extension cannot go past 31.12.2029.

The durability period is three years from the date of the final payment (section 5.1.1.4). Section I art. 1 of the model contract (Annex 15, the programme's common conditions) says 5 years, but section II art. 2 (the conditions of this call) expressly completes it with 3 years, as in the guide. For this call the rule is 3 years; check the text at signing. Durability reports are filed yearly in MySMIS (section 11.1), and cloud subscriptions in this period are paid from own funds. Contracting takes place within 180 days of the call closing (section 8.9).

What changed since the consultation

Verifiable on 18 November 2026: public consultation on the draft guide ran from 28.04.2026 to 20.05.2026. The final guide was published on 30.10.2026 and sets the submission window 02.12.2026 - 02.02.2027, unknown during consultation. The de minimis scheme was approved by the ADR Centru director general's decision no. 488/27.10.2026. This page's status moves from "in consultation" to a call open in that window.

What we cannot say: we have not compared the final guide with the consultation draft line by line, and the final guide contains no change log. So we do not claim which rule changed. If you worked from April or May 2026 figures, replace them with the ones on this page, from the final guide.

What Northdan does in this call and what it does not

Northdan Soft SRL is based in Aiud, Alba County, inside the Centru Region, and works with firms that apply; we do not apply ourselves, as our CAEN codes are in Annex 3. On a project we can be the software supplier, meaning ERP, CRM, process automation, online shop or e-invoicing integration, or the digital maturity auditor, or the author of the digitalization plan. We cannot be two of them on the same project, because the rule in sections 5.3.2.8 and 5.3.2.11 forbids it. You can buy the audit from us and the software from someone else, or the software from us and the audit from another authorised auditor or an EDIH.

We do not write the funding application, do not run European project management and do not do the financial audit. We never promise approval. We publish no audit price; for an offer the company sends its field of activity, county and the processes it wants to digitalize, and we answer in writing with the role we can take and the roles you must buy elsewhere. Software offers follow the format of the cost-justification summary, for which the grid awards points for at least 3 verifiable sources (Annex 4, 2.3 a).

Frequently asked questions

When are applications filed for call 2 SME digitalization in Centru?

From 02.12.2026, 12:00, to 02.02.2027, 12:00, exclusively in MySMIS2021 (guide, sections 4.3.1, 4.3.2 and 4.4). Projects filed by 02.01.2027, 24:00, go to contracting with at least 85 points, if they fit within the allocation; those with 50-84 points stay in competition with month 2 if allocation remains; below 50 the project is rejected (section 8.4).

Is it better to file in December or in January?

December, if the file is complete and the 2025 figures keep you eligible with the same score as the 2026 ones (our reading): a project filed by 02.01.2027, 24:00, with at least 85 points goes to contracting if it fits the allocation, while one with 50-84 stays in competition with the January ones, if allocation remains (guide, section 8.4). In January, the reference year for profit and employees becomes 2026 (5.1.1.6, 5.1.1.7).

Which financial statements do I attach if I file in January 2027?

The guide asks for the annual statements approved by the general meeting for the financial year before submission (section 7.4, point 2), meaning 2026 for an application filed in 2027. The guide does not say whether those for 2025 are accepted until 2026 is approved; the question goes to helpdesk@adrcentru.ro, answered within 10 working days at most (section 1.1).

Does the hour I file the application matter?

Yes, in three situations: the hour decides whether the application falls in month 1 (by 02.01.2027, 24:00) or month 2, whether it is in time at all (closing is 02.02.2027, 12:00), and the tie-break at equal score, by criteria 1, 3, 2 and 4 (guide, section 8.4). The hour adds no points.

How many points do I need to get funding?

The quality threshold is 50 points out of 100 (sections 3.1 and 8.5). A project filed in the first month, by 02.01.2027, 24:00, goes to contracting with at least 85 points, after the whole session is evaluated and the appeals are settled, if it fits the allocation; with 50-84 points it stays in competition with month-2 projects (section 8.4).

How much co-financing is required, and is 20% worth it?

At least 10% of eligible value; funding is at most 90% (sections 3.4 and 5.5). Co-financing above the minimum earns points: 4 for exactly 20%, 8 above 20% up to 25%, 12 above 25%, against 1 point at 10%-15% (Annex 4, criterion 3.1). The guide recommends no 20% figure.

Which CAEN code must I not hold to apply?

None of those in Annex 3 activated at submission and no revenue from excluded activities in the last fiscal year and the current year (section 5.1.1.1). The list covers IT (6201, 6202, 6209, 5829, 6311) but also agriculture, fishing, part of the food industry (meat, dairy, milling, sugar etc.), food shops (4711, 4721-4729), tobacco, waste, fuels, gambling and others. Bread and pastry (1071) or coffee (1083), for example, are not on the list.

Must the company have made a profit last year?

Yes: net or operating profit above 0 lei in the fiscal year before submission, plus at least one full fiscal year of activity (section 5.1.1.6). The guide's example covers 2026 submissions only: established by 3 January 2025 and profit at 31 December 2025.

How many employees do I need?

At least one employee on average in the previous fiscal year, or at least one full-time employee on an open-ended contract, at submission and at contracting (section 5.1.1.7).

Who may perform the digital maturity audit?

A legal entity authorised for CAEN 6202 (Rev.2) or 6220 (Rev.3), or an EDIH, independent of the company and of the suppliers it audits (section 5.3.2.11). The author of the digitalization plan can be neither the solution supplier nor the author of the audit report in the same project (section 5.3.2.8).

What does the digital maturity audit cost, and is it eligible?

The guide sets no ceiling for the audit; it is an eligible base activity, and the initial audit done before submission is reimbursable if not before 01.01.2021 (sections 5.3.1 and 5.3.2.11). Each auditor sets the price in its offer.

Can software and cloud subscriptions be reimbursed?

Yes, temporary subscription licences are eligible (section 5.2.2), and cloud, SaaS, PaaS and IaaS only for the implementation period, with durability-period costs from own funds (section 5.3.2.5).

How long does the project last and how long must the investments be kept?

Implementation lasts at most 24 months (section 5.6) and durability is three years from the final payment (section 5.1.1.4). Bought software is kept at least 3 years after project completion (section 5.3.2.1).

Can I buy software before submitting?

No: costs of starting the investment before submission are ineligible, and starting means the first binding order (section 5.3.3). Only application consultancy, the digitalization plan and the initial audit can be bought earlier (section 5.3.1).

Does Northdan write the funding application?

No. On one project Northdan is either the software supplier, or the digital maturity auditor, or the author of the digitalization plan, never two of them, and it does not promise approval.
