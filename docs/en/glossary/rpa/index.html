What is RPA (robotic process automation) and how does it work

Software robots that work inside your existing applications exactly like a person — reading, copying, filling, clicking — without fatigue or slips.

7
sections
5
questions answered

RPA (robotic process automation) is software that carries out, through the applications' own screens, the repetitive steps an employee performs — opening programs, copying data, filling in fields — by fixed rules, without changing the existing systems. Every office has a category of work everyone recognizes and nobody loves: download the report here, copy the columns there, fill in the form in a third system, send, repeat tomorrow. RPA takes over exactly that layer.

The key contrast is with API integration: integration wires systems together underneath but requires them to expose APIs, while RPA works on top, through the same screens people use. That is why it fits where integration cannot reach: legacy applications without APIs, third-party portals, processes crossing several programs. A robot that clicks on screens is more fragile than an API, though, so serious RPA includes supervision. Payback is calculated beforehand: monthly hours saved times hourly cost. Below: how a robot works, examples by department, how RPA differs from APIs, macros and AI agents, the limits, and the Romanian context.

Why it matters for your business

✓Office routine handed to robots

Copying between systems, periodic reports, portal checks — repetitive hours become minutes of supervision.

✓Automation without changing systems

The robot works through the screens of existing applications — including old or third-party ones where API integration is impossible.

✓Slips of attention eliminated

The robot executes the procedure identically on every run — no transposed digits at 5 p.m. and no steps skipped in a hurry.

What RPA means and how a software robot works

The "robot" in RPA is a program running on a computer or a virtual machine, not a physical factory robot. It can be built for a single process, such as picking up a bank statement, or for dozens of processes on the same platform.

Steps are recorded or built in a designer. At run time the robot finds fields and buttons by interface elements, images or coordinates; that is how Microsoft describes Power Automate desktop flows, including on legacy applications and terminal emulators. It starts at a fixed time or on an event such as an incoming e-mail. There are two types: an attended robot runs on the person's computer and starts at their request, an unattended one runs by itself, on a schedule, on a dedicated machine.

RPA examples by department

Finance and accounting: statements downloaded from internet banking and matched with invoices in the accounting program; supplier invoices entered into a program without an import function. Human resources: the same employee data entered into timekeeping, payroll and access accounts; certificates generated from a template.

Operations and logistics: orders picked up from customer portals without an API, waybills created in the courier's application, stock updated in several places. Sales and support: CRM filled from e-mails, weekly reports gathered from three or four sources. Public administration uses the same idea; an example is in the Romania section.

RPA, APIs, macros and AI agents: the difference

The architecture rule: where an API exists, integrate; where none exists, use RPA. A Romanian example: ANAF publishes a web service for the VAT-payer register (article 316 of the Fiscal Code), with at most 100 tax IDs per request and one request per second. Partner checks connect through it, not through a robot filling in the website form.

A macro lives inside a single application, such as Excel, and a script needs a developer and access to the data. RPA crosses several applications through the screen, without access to their databases. An RPA robot follows a fixed scenario. AI that "sees" the screen and decides by itself what to click is a different subject, covered under computer use and autonomous agents.

RPA limits and when it pays off for a business

The fragility is real: when an application updates, the screen changes and the robot can get lost. That is why an implementation includes failure alerts and exception handling: facing a missing value or an unexpected screen, the robot stops cleanly and notifies a person instead of improvising. The robot has its own account with minimal rights, and passwords sit in a password vault, not in the script.

Good processes have clear rules, measurable monthly volume, few exceptions and stable applications. Processes that change monthly or need human judgment do not fit. The economics are calculated beforehand: monthly hours saved times hourly cost, plus maintenance; indicative figures are in the price guide. A vendor proposing RPA where a decent API exists deserves the question why.

RPA in Romania: UiPath, the Ministry of Finance, funding

UiPath, one of the best-known RPA vendors, started in 2005 in Bucharest, according to its own About us page. The Ministry of Finance and UiPath took the first step toward operationalizing the ministry's RPA Center of Excellence, with the stated aim of automating repetitive processes such as issuing certain tax certificates, according to the ministry's press release.

The POCIDIF Action 2.1 Guide (MIPE Order 965 of 23.06.2026), the call for IT firms, listed "RPA platforms and intelligent automation software" among fundable equipment; the call closed on 30 October 2026. Where an RPA robot can be funded today and on which budget line: the dedicated funding page.

Frequently asked questions

What is RPA automation?

RPA automation is a program that repeats, on the screens of applications, the fixed steps of an employee: copying data, filling in forms, downloading reports. It does not replace existing systems but works on top of them. It suits processes with clear rules where people lose hours to repetitive work.

What is an attended and an unattended RPA robot?

An attended robot runs on the person's computer and starts at their request, helping during work. An unattended robot runs by itself, on a schedule or on an event, on a dedicated machine. Licensing differs between the two types; details are in the RPA price guide.

RPA or API integration — which should we choose for our process?

API where one exists, because it is more robust and faster, and RPA where none does: legacy apps, third-party portals, chains across different systems. They are complementary layers, and mature projects combine them. A vendor proposing RPA where a decent API exists deserves the question why.

What does an RPA robot cost?

As a guide, a point automation (a recurring report, a transfer between two applications) costs €1,000–5,000 and RPA across a whole workflow €10,000–30,000, plus 10–15% a year for upkeep. The platform licence is paid separately, by vendor and robot type; the figures and the payback calculation are in the RPA price guide.

What happens when an application the robot uses changes?

The robot stumbles, because the screen no longer looks as before. That is why an implementation includes monitoring with failure alerts, a clean stop on exceptions and maintenance for interface changes. AI tools that "understand" screens reduce the fragility but do not remove the need for supervision.
