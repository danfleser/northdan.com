What is traceability?

Traceability is the ability to trace and follow a product, by batch or serial number, from the raw material and supplier to the customer it was delivered to.

Traceability is the ability to trace and follow a product, by batch or serial number, through every stage of production, processing and distribution: which raw materials it was made from, which supplier they came from, which production run they entered and who received the finished product. In food and feed, Article 18 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 has made it mandatory since 1 January 2005: every operator must be able to identify who supplied it and which businesses it supplied, and make this information available to the authorities on request. Implementing Regulation (EU) No 931/2011 calls the rule “one step back, one step forward”: the direct supplier and the direct business customer, not the final consumer.

A batch has to be withdrawn from the market and the authority asks which raw materials it was made from and which customers received it. Companies with batch records produce the answer from a report; the rest stop production and start leafing through paper registers. The difference is between a surgical recall, the affected batch and its customers and nothing more, and one in which you call back everything sold because the problem cannot be isolated. The cost of the discipline is small and daily; the cost of its absence is rare and occasionally fatal to the company that skipped it.

Why it matters for your business

Recalls limited to the affected batch

Identifying the exact runs with a problem and the customers who received them reduces a recall from everything shipped this quarter to a few pallets, with costs to match.

Compliance you can demonstrate

Food safety audits, quality certifications and inspections receive documented answers in minutes, which large customers increasingly require before signing a contract at all.

Root causes found at source

When a non-conformity appears, the chain from batch to raw material to supplier to machine shows immediately where it originated, turning a complaint into a process improvement.

The one-step-back, one-step-forward rule

Article 18 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 requires traceability at all stages of production, processing and distribution for food, feed, food-producing animals and any substance incorporated into them. An operator must be able to identify its supplier, have systems and procedures that show which businesses it supplied, give this information to the competent authority on request, and label or identify products so that traceability is easy. The duty covers the supplier and business customers, not the final consumer.

For food of animal origin, Implementing Regulation (EU) No 931/2011, applicable from 1 July 2012, requires eight items per batch, passed to the buyer and, on request, to the authority: the precise description of the food, its volume or quantity, the name and address of the operator it was dispatched from, the consignor or owner if different, the name and address of the operator it was dispatched to, the consignee or owner if different, the batch or consignment reference, and the date of dispatch. The data is updated daily and kept at least until the food can reasonably be assumed to have been consumed.

In Romania the checks are carried out by ANSVSA, which publishes its traceability-system evaluation procedure on ansvsa.ro.

The batch: definition and the “L” mark

Under Directive 2011/91/EU, a batch is a set of sales units produced, manufactured or packaged in practically identical circumstances. The batch code is set by the producer, packer or first seller established in the Union and is preceded by the letter “L”, unless it is clearly distinguishable from the other indications on the label. Marking is not required when the label carries the minimum durability date or the use-by date with at least the day and month uncoded, in that order.

A serial number identifies a single unit, a batch identifies a group of units. A batch is enough for most foods; a serial number is used where the individual item matters.

GS1 codes for traceability

On packs and pallets, traceability data is encoded with GS1 Application Identifiers: (01) GTIN, the product code, 14 digits; (10) batch or lot, up to 20 alphanumeric characters; (17) expiry date, 6 digits in YYMMDD format; (21) serial number, up to 20 characters; (00) SSCC, the logistic unit code (for example a pallet), 18 digits, on a GS1-128 label; (414) GLN, the location number. GS1 Romania is the official source for GS1 identifiers with the 594 prefix. On 20 July 2026 GS1 Romania announced a pilot with more than 50 companies preparing the move to GS1 QR codes.

Medicines have their own rules: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/161, applicable from 9 February 2019, requires a unique identifier on each pack of prescription medicines (and a few non-prescription ones listed in Annex II) with the product code, the serial number (at most 20 characters, randomised), the national reimbursement number where required, the batch number and the expiry date, in a Data Matrix code. Medical devices use UDI, made of a UDI-DI (the device) and a UDI-PI (the production unit), under Article 27 of Regulation (EU) 2017/745.

Types of traceability

Backward (upstream) traceability starts from the product and reaches the supplier and the ingredient batch. Forward (downstream) traceability starts from a batch and reaches the customers who received it. Internal traceability links, inside the plant, the ingredient batch to the production run and to the finished-goods batch.

The word has another meaning too: metrological traceability, the calibration chain of a measuring instrument. This page does not cover it.

How a company keeps traceability in an ERP or WMS

Technically, traceability lives inside the ERP or the warehouse management system: goods receipts recorded per batch with supplier and expiry date, consumption booked against production runs, labels generated from the product record, and a scan at every movement. A periodic mock recall shows whether a batch can be traced in both directions.

For non-food products, Regulation (EU) 2023/988 on general product safety, applicable from 13 December 2024, requires under Article 15 that every economic operator can identify, on request, who supplied the product (or a part, component or embedded software) and which operator it supplied it to, and can present this for 6 years.

Frequently asked questions

Which companies are legally required to maintain traceability?⌄

Food and feed operators (Reg. 178/2002, Art. 18), with the eight batch data items for food of animal origin (Reg. 931/2011), prescription medicines (Delegated Reg. 2016/161), medical devices (Reg. 2017/745, Art. 27) and consumer products in general, on request of the authority (Reg. 2023/988, Art. 15). Large retail customers frequently impose contractual requirements stricter than the statutory minimum.

What is the difference between batch and serial traceability?⌄

A batch groups products made under the same conditions — the same run, day and recipe — and is the usual level in food. A serial number uniquely identifies each individual item and is used where the single unit matters: electronics, equipment, medical devices. The management system has to be chosen and configured for the level your industry requires, because moving from batch to serial later is expensive and disruptive.

Can we keep traceability in a spreadsheet?⌄

In theory at very small volumes; in practice manual records fail exactly when they matter. Consumption gets noted after the fact, links between batch and product are lost during busy periods, and a recall becomes a reconstruction from memory. A system with batch management records the links automatically at receipt, production and dispatch, and barcode scanning removes typing errors from the chain as well.

What does “one step back, one step forward” mean?⌄

Each operator knows its direct supplier and its direct business customer, not the whole chain and not the final consumer. The full chain is rebuilt by linking these steps to one another, from farm to shelf. The wording appears in recital 3 of Regulation (EU) No 931/2011, and the rule in Article 18(2)–(3) of Regulation 178/2002.

What does the letter “L” on a package mean?⌄

“L” precedes the batch code, set by the producer, packer or first seller in the Union (Directive 2011/91/EU, Art. 3). The letter may be omitted if the batch is clearly distinguishable from other indications. Marking is also not required when the label shows the minimum durability or use-by date with the day and month uncoded.

How long must traceability records be kept?⌄

There is no single period for every sector. For food of animal origin, the data is updated daily and kept at least until the food can reasonably be assumed to have been consumed (Reg. 931/2011, Art. 3). For consumer products in general, supplier and customer data can be presented for 6 years (Reg. 2023/988, Art. 15).

Which GS1 codes are used for batch and expiry date?⌄

Identifier (01) is the product GTIN, (10) the batch, (17) the expiry date in YYMMDD format and (21) the serial number. A pallet carries an SSCC, identifier (00), on a GS1-128 label. GS1 Romania is the official source for GS1 identifiers with the 594 prefix.
