What is VoIP?

VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol) is telephony that sends voice as data packets over the internet instead of the classic telephone network.

VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol) is telephony in which the voice is turned into data packets and sent over the internet or the company network: SIP sets up and ends the call (RFC 3261) and RTP carries the sound (RFC 3550). You place and receive calls to and from any fixed or mobile number, and the other party sees no difference. In Romania, geographic numbers (021 for Bucharest, 0264 for Cluj) can also work outside their county since 13 December 2019, if the provider offers it (ANCOM Decision 1069/2018), and 037x numbers are location-independent from the start (National Numbering Plan).

For a business, VoIP usually means a virtual exchange: numbers, an extension for every colleague, a voice menu (“press 1 for sales”), different routing inside and outside working hours, waiting queues and call recording, with the notification duty attached to it. CRM integration opens the customer record on a call, logs the conversation against the contact and shows missed calls. The same extension rings the same way at the office, at home or in a second site, because the phone is an app or an IP handset connected to the internet.

Why it matters for your business

✓A company exchange without an exchange

Numbers, extensions, menus and routing administered from a web page: the capabilities of large corporations on a small-business subscription.

✓A distributed office, invisible to customers

The team answers the company numbers from anywhere, so telephony follows the people rather than the building, and remote work is not audible on the line.

✓The phone connected to sales

CRM integration brings up the customer record on every call and logs conversations automatically, so missed calls and response times finally become visible numbers.

How a VoIP call works: SIP and RTP

SIP (Session Initiation Protocol) is, according to RFC 3261 (June 2002), an application-layer signalling protocol for creating, modifying and terminating sessions with one or more participants. The phone registers with the exchange (REGISTER), a call starts with INVITE and ends with BYE. RFC 3261 defines six methods: REGISTER, INVITE, ACK, CANCEL, BYE and OPTIONS.

The voice itself does not travel over SIP. A codec encodes it, for example ITU-T G.711 (the PCM coding of classic telephony) or G.722 (“HD voice”, 7 kHz audio within 64 kbit/s), and it is sent in RTP packets (RFC 3550, July 2003) that are reassembled at the other end.

Encryption does not come by default and is not end to end: SIP signalling can be protected with TLS and the sound with SRTP (RFC 3711, March 2004), on each segment of the path, only if the provider enables them. That is a question for the provider, not an assumption.

VoIP and classic telephony: the difference

A classic fixed line (PSTN) holds a dedicated circuit for the whole call. VoIP shares the same data connection with all other traffic, so quality depends on the network: latency, jitter and lost packets. The router gives voice traffic priority (QoS), so that a file download does not cut the call.

A classic line works without internet; VoIP needs internet or an automatic diversion to a mobile when the connection fails. Otherwise both reach the same numbers, and fixed numbers can be ported in both directions.

Types of VoIP: app, IP phone, virtual exchange, SIP trunk

A softphone is an app on a computer or mobile phone. An IP phone is a desk handset connected directly to the network. An ATA adaptor connects an old analogue phone or fax to a VoIP line. A virtual exchange (cloud PBX) means the operator hosts the exchange and the company administers it from a web page. An on-premise IP exchange runs at the company site. A SIP trunk is the set of lines an operator provides to the company’s own exchange.

Messaging apps (WhatsApp, Teams calls between accounts) also use voice over IP, but between accounts, without a number from the national numbering plan, unless a telephony plan is attached.

Numbers in VoIP: geographic, location-independent, porting

In Romania, the National Numbering Plan (ANCOM Decision 375/2013, amended by 1069/2018 and 71/2023) defines geographic numbers as 02x and 03x with 10 digits; the prefix shows the county: 021 and 031 for Bucharest and Ilfov, 0258 and 0358 for Alba, 0264 and 0364 for Cluj. Numbers 037, 038 and 039 are location-independent, intended mainly for services at fixed points.

ANCOM Decision 1069/2018 (Official Gazette 1058 of 13.12.2018, applicable after 12 months) allows these geographic numbers (the 10-digit ones, with the leading 0) to be used outside their area too, under the conditions of location-independent numbers. The provider chooses whether to offer it. The consequence for 112: a call from such a number may reach the dispatcher of the county where the number is registered, and the operator asks the caller for the location. Staff must know to state the address on a 112 call.

Porting is requested at the new provider. According to ANCOM (21.10.2026), it is usually completed in 3 working days, with a later agreed date of at most 25 days and an interruption of at most 4 hours on the porting day. Since 21.10.2008, 15,535,875 numbers have been ported, of which 1,286,392 fixed. If porting is more than one working day late against the agreed date, the subscriber can claim compensation of 5 lei per number per day, capped at 250 lei per day above 50 numbers, within 60 days (ANCOM Decision 72/2023). This is compensation set by law, not a service price.

What a business checks before moving to VoIP

The connection: internet with spare bandwidth, QoS for voice and an automatic diversion to mobile, configured in advance, for outages. The transition: a new number tested for a few weeks, then porting of the main numbers.

Questions for the provider: can my geographic number work outside the county (Decision 1069/2018)? Are SIP over TLS and SRTP enabled? Is there integration with my CRM? How is call recording done and the GDPR notice given? How is the location passed on 112 calls? Are per-minute tariffs transparent and written in the contract? Porting is a subscriber’s right, not a favour from the provider.

Frequently asked questions

Can we keep our existing phone numbers when moving to internet telephony?

Yes. Number portability is a regulated right in Romania: you request it at the new provider and it usually takes 3 working days, with at most 4 hours without service on the porting day (ANCOM). The number stays the same for customers; schedule it for a day and time with few calls. Test the new service on a new number for a few weeks, then port the main numbers.

What happens to the phones when the internet connection fails?

The exchange lives in the cloud, so it keeps working — only your office has dropped out of the equation. Calls divert automatically to mobiles under a rule configured in advance rather than during the crisis, voicemail catches what cannot be answered, and the provider’s mobile apps keep extensions alive over a cellular connection. With a cheap second connection or router failover the scenario becomes almost theoretical, but the diversion rule gets configured regardless, because it costs nothing.

Is recording customer calls legal, and how is it done properly?

Yes, with a legal basis and transparency. An announcement at the start of the call (“this call may be recorded for...”) is mandatory, and GDPR Article 13 requires information at the point of collection. The purpose must be legitimate and documented, access restricted and retention limited in time. Voice is personal data, so recording enters the company’s record of processing activities.

What does VoIP mean?

VoIP stands for Voice over Internet Protocol, that is, telephony over the internet. The voice is turned into data packets that travel over the internet or the company network instead of through a dedicated telephone circuit. In Romanian it is called “voce peste IP”.

What is SIP and what is a SIP trunk?

SIP is the protocol that initiates, modifies and ends calls (RFC 3261). A SIP trunk is a set of telephone lines an operator provides over the internet to the company’s own exchange, instead of classic physical lines. The company keeps its exchange, and the operator supplies the numbers and the calls.

Can a company in Alba have a Bucharest number?

Yes, if the provider offers the use of geographic numbers outside their area (ANCOM Decision 1069/2018, applicable since 13.12.2019) or if the company picks a 037x number, which is location-independent. The provider is not obliged to offer it, so ask before signing the contract.

What internet do you need for VoIP?

Bandwidth rarely limits it: G.711 and G.722 encode voice at 64 kbit/s per direction, plus packet headers. Quality is decided by connection stability (latency, jitter, lost packets) and QoS on the router. A backup connection or diversion to mobile covers outages.
