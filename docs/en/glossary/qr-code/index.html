The QR code (Quick Response): how it works and where it is used

The square barcode a phone reads with its camera: what it holds, why it works, when it breaks, and what data a Romanian fiscal receipt must carry in it.

7
sections
7
questions answered

A QR code (Quick Response) is a two-dimensional barcode, a matrix of black squares on a white background that a phone camera reads from any angle; the Japanese company Denso Wave released it in 1994 for factories that needed to store more data than a classic barcode could hold. The international standard is ISO/IEC 18004, from June 2000. Denso Wave holds the patent but has chosen not to exercise it for standardized codes, which is why static QR code generators are free.

For a company, a QR code is the cheapest link between a physical object and a web page: from the table to the menu, from the invoice to the payment page, from the label to the warranty certificate, from a poster to a registration form. Two things separate good use from poor use. The destination must be a page designed for mobile, because a heavy PDF that nobody can read on a phone cancels the effect. And for anything printed in volume you choose a dynamic code, which lets you change the destination after printing and count the scans. Checked on 18 November 2026 against qrcode.com (Denso Wave), Romanian Government Emergency Ordinances (OUG) 28/1999 and 43/2026.

Why it matters for your business

✓Physical surfaces become clickable

Packaging, tables, posters, company vehicles — any printed surface becomes a direct entrance to your site, menu, payment page or offer.

✓Offline marketing finally measurable

Dynamic codes count scans per placement, so you learn whether the trade-fair flyer or the window poster actually brings people in.

✓Almost zero friction

No mistyped addresses and no searching: the customer lands within seconds on precisely the page you intended them to see.

How a QR code works

Three corners of the square carry position detection patterns, nested squares with a black-to-white ratio of 1:1:3:1:1. The Denso Wave developers chose that ratio because it is the least common one in printed material, so the camera finds the code from any angle, does not mistake an image for it, and reads it more than 10 times faster than other codes of the time (official history, qrcode.com).

Capacity grows with the version: there are 40 versions, from 21×21 to 177×177 modules, with 4 more modules per side at each step. The first QR code held about 7,000 digits plus Kanji characters. Error correction has four levels, L, M, Q and H, which can restore roughly 7%, 15%, 25% and 30% of the data; level M is the most used. That is why a dirty or slightly scratched code still scans.

The code needs a blank margin (the quiet zone) of 4 modules on every side, or the camera cannot separate it from the background. What it can hold: a link, Wi-Fi credentials, a contact card (vCard), plain text or payment data.

QR code versus classic barcode

A classic line barcode holds only about 20 alphanumeric characters, which is why Denso Wave created the QR code. In practice, the barcode at the till identifies a product in the shop's database, while a QR code carries the data or a link itself, so it can send a customer to a page, an app or a payment.

Static and dynamic QR codes

A static code contains the final address written into the pattern. It is free and works as long as that address exists, but it cannot be changed after printing and does not count scans. A dynamic code contains a short address on a redirect service, which you can point to another page at any time and which counts scans; the service is usually paid, and if you close it or stop paying, printed codes lead nowhere.

Decision rule: for a business card or an address that never changes, use a static code; for packaging, posters, flyers or printed menus in volume, use a dynamic one, ideally with UTM parameters so your analytics show which placement brings visitors. Code size is the number of modules multiplied by the size of one module, so a short link gives fewer modules and a code that stays readable even when small. Print it as large as the space allows; "at least 2×2 cm" is only a rule of thumb for close-range reading on a business card or label, not a standard.

The QR code on Romanian fiscal receipts

In Romania, Emergency Ordinance (OUG) 69/2024 (art. V point 2) added art. 4 para. (2¹) to OUG 28/1999: the date and time of issue of the fiscal receipt, the receipt identification number and the fiscal series of the cash register must also be printed as a QR code. The code on the receipt therefore does not lead to the shop's website or a payment; it carries those three data.

The penalty for not having the code is suspended. The fine under art. 11 para. (1) letter h) of OUG 28/1999 for a receipt without these data was suspended first by OUG 125/2024, then by art. IV of OUG 68/2025 until 1 November 2026. The Government extended the suspension through OUG 43/2026, adopted on 30 October 2026, until 31 October 2027 inclusive. So fines did not start in November 2026, and the obligation itself has not been repealed.

The reason, from the preamble of OUG 43/2026: secondary and tertiary rules for cash registers still had to be changed, authorised distributors had to write the software, models had to be retested for the technical approval supplement, and the new software had to be installed on about 1,000,000 cash registers. For a merchant the rule is simple: do not paste a home-made code on receipts. The code comes from the software update of the authorised distributor, for an approved cash register model; ask the distributor when the update arrives.

QR codes in business: payments, time tracking, orders

Payments: in Romania, RoPay, the instant-payment scheme launched on 14 October 2024 by TransFonD together with the Romanian Banking Association, lets a payer scan a standard QR code in the bank's mobile app; the central bank (BNR) approved the scheme rules on 5 July 2024, and the money reaches the merchant in at most 10 seconds. It works only between participating banks.

Time tracking: the employee scans a code at the entrance, or a reader scans the employee's code, and the app records the time. The details are on the time-tracking page.

On-demand orders: at Resort Buggy (resortbuggy.com), the guest scans a QR code, requests the buggy and follows it on a live map, with no app installed. The same pattern works for table menus, warranties on a label or sign-up forms on a poster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between static and dynamic QR codes?

A static code contains the final address and cannot be changed after printing; it is free, and static generators are free because Denso Wave does not exercise its patent. A dynamic one routes through an address you can redirect and which counts scans. For printed runs, choose dynamic.

Can QR codes be used for payments?

Yes. In Romania, RoPay, launched by TransFonD in October 2024, allows an instant payment by scanning a QR code in a mobile banking app; the money reaches the merchant in at most 10 seconds if both banks take part. A QR on an invoice can also lead to a card payment page.

Why is nobody scanning our QR codes?

Usually the chain is broken rather than the code: no reason is given (scan for the menu, a discount or the warranty), the destination is not mobile-friendly, or the code is too small or badly placed. Print it as large as the space allows, with a 4-module blank margin and good contrast.

What data does the QR code on a Romanian fiscal receipt contain?

The date and time of issue, the receipt identification number and the fiscal series of the cash register, under OUG 28/1999, art. 4 para. (2¹). The fine for lacking it is suspended until 31 October 2027 inclusive by OUG 43/2026, so the obligation exists but is not yet sanctioned.

How does time tracking with a QR code work?

The employee scans a code at the entrance with a phone, or a reader scans the employee's personal code, and the app saves the time. In Romania, the Labour Code (art. 119 para. 1) requires daily working hours with start and end times to be recorded. A static wall code can be photographed, so use codes that change every few seconds or a location check.

How do you scan a QR code with a phone?

Open the Camera app, on iPhone and on most Android phones, and point it at the code until a link appears; on other models use Google Lens. Before opening the link, read the address the phone shows: if it does not look like what you expected, do not open it.

Is it safe to scan any QR code?

No. A sticker placed over a real code can lead to a fake payment or sign-in page (phishing). Read the address the phone shows before opening it, and never enter card details or a password after a scan you did not ask for.
