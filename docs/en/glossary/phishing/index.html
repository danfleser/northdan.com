IT Glossary

What is phishing and how does a company defend against it?

Electronic fraud by message: impersonating trusted senders — banks, colleagues, suppliers — to steal your passwords, data or money.

Phishing is fraud in which an attacker poses as a trusted sender — a bank, the tax authority, a courier, a colleague or Microsoft — in an email, text, call or chat message, to get you to type your password into a cloned page, open an infected file or pay into the attacker's account. In the ENISA Threat Landscape 2025 report (4,875 incidents in the EU, July 2024 – June 2025), phishing — including vishing, malspam and malvertising — appears as the way in for about 60% of the cases analysed. It breaks no software: it persuades you to open the door yourself.

For companies, the prized targets are employee mailboxes and payment workflows. A compromised account becomes a launchpad: the attacker reads real correspondence, learns the relationships, then strikes clients and suppliers in your name, inside genuine conversations. Phishing succeeds not where people are stupid but where they are rushed — real defense makes verification easier than haste.

Why it matters for your business

Accounts survive stolen passwords

Passkeys and security keys (FIDO) do not work on a cloned page, so a phished password or code no longer reaches the attacker; SMS codes remain better than nothing.

Trained reflexes, not just warnings

Periodic simulations with immediate feedback turn verification into a habit, not a slogan: sender address, link, request for money.

Payments procedurally immunized

Confirming any account change or unusual payment on a separate channel cuts off exactly the fraud that hurts most — the irreversible transfer.

How a phishing attack works

The attack has three steps. The bait: an urgent message impersonating a known sender ("your parcel is waiting for a delivery fee", "your account expires today"). The trap: a link to a cloned login page or an infected attachment. The theft: the password, code or card details typed into the fake page go straight to the attacker.

Inside a company the chain goes further. With one employee's password the attacker enters the mailbox and often sets silent forwarding rules to receive a copy of the correspondence. Then they wait for a real conversation with a supplier and send, from within it, an invoice "with a changed IBAN".

The consequences are taken-over accounts, irreversible payments, exposed client data (with GDPR obligations) and malware installed through attachments. The types of malware are explained separately, under the malware term.

Types of phishing: spear phishing, smishing, vishing, quishing, BEC

Spear phishing — a message aimed at a specific person, researched from public sources and flawlessly written, often with AI. When the target is an executive, it is called whaling.

Smishing — phishing by text message (and by WhatsApp or Telegram): "your parcel is waiting", "you have received a refund".

Vishing — phishing by voice call, including cloned voices: "this is your bank, your account is at risk".

Quishing — a link hidden in a QR code, which email filters read less easily than a written link.

BEC (Business Email Compromise) — fraud with an altered invoice or payment order, sent from the real account of a colleague or supplier. "CEO fraud" schemes are described under the social engineering term.

How to recognize a phishing message

Typos are no longer a giveaway: messages written with AI are grammatically correct. Four checks are hard to fake.

The sender's real address, not the display name. The domain behind the link — "anaf-ro.info" is not ANAF; hover over the link before clicking. The nature of the request: urgency plus a sensitive action (login, payment, data) means verification on another channel, however authentic the message looks. And the golden rule: no password is ever typed on a page opened from a received link.

Common patterns in Romania: "your parcel is waiting for a delivery fee", "ANAF / SPV refund", "your Microsoft 365 password expires today", "the supplier has changed its bank account".

Defense in a company: phishing-resistant 2FA, reflexes, SPF/DKIM/DMARC

Two-factor authentication: any second factor beats none, but codes sent by SMS or generated in an app can be typed into a fake page that relays them to the attacker in real time. The US agency CISA (fact sheet "Implementing Phishing-Resistant MFA", October 2022) says the only widely available phishing-resistant authentication is FIDO/WebAuthn: passkeys and security keys are bound to the real domain and do not work on a clone.

Reflexes: links opened from a bookmark or an app, not from a message; payments and account changes confirmed by phone, on the number in the contract; periodic simulations with feedback; an internal button or address for "I received something strange", with no shame for whoever reports.

Protecting your own domain: since February 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require every sender to have SPF or DKIM, and senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail need SPF and DKIM, a DMARC record (the policy may be p=none), alignment of the "From" domain and one-click unsubscribe. Outlook.com (hotmail, live, outlook) requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC, at least p=none, from domains sending more than 5,000 messages a day, and since 5 May 2025 rejects non-compliant mail with error 550 5.7.515. Gmail announced it is ramping up enforcement from November 2025, with temporary and permanent rejections.

What most guides miss: p=none only produces reports. Impersonation of your domain stops only when the policy moves to quarantine or reject, after the reports show that all legitimate senders pass — exactly the rollout Google Workspace recommends. The step-by-step setup is explained in the question about company email landing in spam.

Where to report phishing in Romania

Report to DNSC (the National Cyber Security Directorate): on the 1911 line, available 24/7, or on the PNRISC platform (pnrisc.dnsc.ro), the national platform for reporting cybersecurity incidents. The platform states that the report does not replace a criminal complaint.

If money has left the account, file a complaint with the Police and tell the bank immediately (card block, transfer recall). In Gmail, mark the message with "Report phishing", in the menu next to "Reply". Inside a company, employees need a single address or an internal button where they send suspicious messages.

Frequently asked questions

How do I recognize a well-crafted phishing email?⌄

Check the anchors that are hard to fake, not typos: the sender's real address (not the display name), the domain behind the link (hover before clicking) and the nature of the request. Urgency plus a login, payment or data request means verification on another channel, however authentic the message looks.

An employee clicked and entered their password — what are the first actions?⌄

Change the account's password (and anywhere it was reused), invalidate active sessions, check the mailbox's forwarding rules, verify 2FA and notify IT. If money has left, call the bank immediately. No witch hunts: whoever reports within five minutes limits the damage.

How do I stop criminals from sending email in my company's name?⌄

Configure SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain. Start with p=none to read the reports, then move to quarantine or reject. Gmail and Yahoo have required SPF or DKIM from all senders since February 2024, and DMARC from bulk senders (at Gmail: more than 5,000 messages a day).

Where do I report phishing in Romania?⌄

To DNSC, on 1911 or on the PNRISC platform; if you lost money, also file a complaint with the Police and tell the bank immediately, because a DNSC report does not replace a criminal complaint. In Gmail, mark the message with "Report phishing".

What is the difference between phishing and smishing?⌄

Smishing is phishing by text message (or WhatsApp/Telegram): the same goal — password, card details, payment — but the channel is the phone, where the link is shortened and the real address is hard to see. The rule is the same: do not open the received link; go to the courier's or bank's app yourself.
