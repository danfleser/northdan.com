Core Web Vitals: Google's three page-experience metrics

LCP, INP and CLS: loading, response to a click and page stability, with Google's official thresholds and the difference between real-user and lab data.

Core Web Vitals are the three metrics Google uses to measure real experience on a page: LCP (Largest Contentful Paint, how long the main content takes to appear) should be 2.5 seconds or less, INP (Interaction to Next Paint, how quickly the page reacts to a click or tap) 200 milliseconds or less, and CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift, how much content jumps around) 0.1 or less. The thresholds apply at the 75th percentile of visits, separately on mobile and desktop, and INP replaced the old FID (First Input Delay) on 12 March 2024 (thresholds checked on 18 November 2026 at web.dev). CLS is the one everyone feels: nobody wants to reach for a button and hit the advert that has just pushed it aside.

The scores are public and free to measure, so you can check the technical quality of a delivered site objectively instead of taking anyone's word for it. A properly built site passes the lab tests from launch, and the real-user values confirm it only once there is traffic. Common causes of weak scores, and their fixes, are in the articles listed below.

Why it matters for your business

A tie-breaker in search when content is equal

When several pages answer a search equally well, Google can favour the better experience; the relevance of the content remains the main criterion.

Conversions saved from abandonment

Visitors do not wait: improvements in real-world speed show up as a percentage change in completed baskets and submitted forms.

An objective check on your supplier

The scores are public and measurable, so you can verify the technical quality of a delivered site in black and white instead of taking anyone's word for it.

The three metrics and their thresholds

LCP measures how long the largest image or text block in the initial screen takes to render: good at 2.5 seconds or less, needs improvement between 2.5 and 4 seconds, poor above 4 seconds. INP measures the time from a tap or click until the page repaints: good at 200 ms or less, needs improvement between 200 and 500 ms, poor above 500 ms. CLS adds up how much elements move unexpectedly: good at 0.1 or less, needs improvement between 0.1 and 0.25, poor above 0.25.

The 75th percentile means at least 75 of every 100 visits must reach the good threshold, and a page passes the assessment only if all three metrics reach it. FID measured only the delay of the first interaction; INP looks at the interactions across the whole visit, which is why it is stricter.

Field data and lab data

Field data comes from CrUX (the Chrome User Experience Report): real Chrome users over the last 28 days. It feeds Google's assessment and the Search Console report. Lab data is a Lighthouse simulation on a mid-range phone, useful for diagnosis.

The 0–100 score in PageSpeed Insights is the Lighthouse lab score, not the Core Web Vitals assessment: a site can show 95 and still fail on real data, or the reverse. INP cannot be measured in the lab because it needs real interactions; Lighthouse uses TBT (Total Blocking Time) as a proxy.

A small site with little traffic may have no field data. If only INP data is missing, PageSpeed Insights judges the page on LCP and CLS alone.

How much they count for Google rankings

Google says its ranking systems use Core Web Vitals, but there is no single page-experience signal. Google always seeks to show the most relevant content, even if the page experience is sub-par; for many queries, though, there is a lot of helpful content, and a great page experience can contribute to success in those cases (Google Search documentation, "Page experience", updated 22 September 2026).

In practice: good scores do not replace relevant content, but when two pages answer equally well, experience can tip the balance.

How to check, and how to confirm a fix

In Google Search Console, the Core Web Vitals report groups similar URLs, and a group's status is set by its weakest metric. Only indexed URLs appear, with mobile and desktop separate. "No data available" means a new property or too few visitors. After a fix, "Start tracking" starts a 28-day validation. PageSpeed Insights shows a single URL with both field and lab data.

At handover, ask for a pass at the 75th percentile on mobile in field data, not a screenshot of a lab score of 100. Causes and fixes are in the articles listed below.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I see my Core Web Vitals scores?⌄

Free, in two places: PageSpeed Insights for any single address, showing both lab and real-user data, and the dedicated report in Google Search Console for the whole site, grouped by page type. What counts for ranking is the field data collected from actual visitors.

What most often ruins a company site's scores?⌄

A predictable list: unoptimised images uploaded at print resolution, dozens of marketing and chat scripts accumulated over the years, fonts that block rendering, cheap slow hosting, and banners that shove content around. A technical audit takes them in order, and the first two usually deliver the biggest jump.

Do perfect scores mean first place in Google?⌄

No. Relevant content and domain authority weigh more; Google shows the most relevant page even when the experience is weak. Core Web Vitals can help when several pages answer equally well, so they are hygiene and a tie-breaker, not a magic wand.

What replaced FID in Core Web Vitals?⌄

INP (Interaction to Next Paint), on 12 March 2024. FID measured only the delay of the first interaction, while INP measures the latency of interactions across the whole visit, up to the next repaint. The good threshold for INP is 200 ms or less.

Does a score of 100 in PageSpeed Insights mean I pass Core Web Vitals?⌄

No. The 0–100 score comes from a Lighthouse lab simulation. The Core Web Vitals assessment uses real visitor data at the 75th percentile over the last 28 days, so the lab score and the assessment can disagree.

Why is the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console empty?⌄

Usually the property is new or the site lacks enough Chrome visitors for CrUX data; only indexed URLs appear. Until then, use the lab data in PageSpeed Insights and test on a phone, then come back once traffic builds up.

How long until a Core Web Vitals fix shows up?⌄

Gradually, over up to 28 days, because field data covers the last 28 days of visits. Search Console validation ("Start tracking") also monitors for 28 days, so do not expect an overnight change.
