What is spyware?

Spyware is a type of malware that installs itself in secret and sends someone else what it sees on a device: passwords, keystrokes, messages, files, location.

Spyware is software secretly installed on a computer or phone that collects and sends someone else passwords, keystrokes, messages, files or location without the user's knowledge. The Romanian term is „program spion”. It belongs to the malware family but, unlike ransomware, which announces itself, it works only while it stays unseen, so it rarely produces symptoms. The scale shows in public figures: in just two months, between 16 March and 16 May 2025, Microsoft identified over 394,000 Windows computers infected with a single infostealer, Lumma, before it was disrupted in a joint action with Europol and the US Department of Justice.

For a company, one infected workstation means the passwords saved in the browser and the session cookies of every account used on it. With a session cookie an attacker enters the account without the password and without passing two-factor authentication again. The defence therefore rests on architecture (a password manager, two-factor authentication, restricted install rights), on endpoint detection and response on workstations, and on watching the accounts from the outside; the sections below give the detail, together with the legal limit on monitoring employees.

Why it matters for your business

✓Company passwords out of harvesting range

A password manager plus two-factor authentication mean an infostealer reaching one workstation leaves with far less; architecture limits the damage before detection happens.

✓Exfiltration caught by behaviour

Modern endpoint detection can spot suspicious collection and transmission even from previously unseen variants — precisely what signature-based antivirus used to miss.

✓Accounts watched from the outside

Periodic review of logins and active sessions catches the consequence of spying, which is foreign access, even when the infection itself stayed invisible.

How spyware gets in and what it takes

The most common entry paths are pirated software and cracks, email attachments, poisoned adverts (malvertising) and compromised websites, browser extensions and physical access to a phone. Mercenary spyware can also arrive with no click at all, by exploiting a vulnerability in the phone.

What it takes: passwords saved in the browser, session cookies, cards, crypto wallets, documents, screenshots and, for some types, the microphone and camera. Microsoft Incident Response described the „pass-the-cookie” scenario in 2022: a stolen session cookie compromises the resource without the attacker having to satisfy multifactor authentication.

Types of spyware

Infostealer: takes everything saved on the workstation on first run and leaves. Lumma, for example, steals, according to Microsoft, passwords, credit cards, bank accounts and cryptocurrency wallets, and has been sold as a service on forums since at least 2022.

Keylogger: records keystrokes, either as software or as a USB device placed between keyboard and computer.

Remote access trojan (RAT): gives the attacker full control of the workstation, including camera and microphone.

Stalkerware: surveillance applications installed on somebody else's phone; it has its own section below.

Data-collecting adware: tracks browsing for adverts and becomes spyware when it sells the data without the user's consent.

Mercenary spyware: for example Pegasus, from NSO Group, sold to states and used, according to Apple, against a very small number of individuals, often journalists, activists, politicians and diplomats. Apple sends „threat notifications” to those targeted and recommends Lockdown Mode.

Stalkerware: spying on a phone

Stalkerware is a commercially sold application, often marketed as „parental control” or „monitoring”, installed by someone with physical access to the phone, such as a partner or family member. It runs hidden and sends location, messages and calls (Kaspersky).

If you suspect stalkerware, removing it can alert the person who installed it and can destroy evidence. In an abusive relationship a safety plan with a support organisation comes first; the Coalition Against Stalkerware, founded in November 2019, lists such organisations at stopstalkerware.org.

In Romania, secretly installing it on someone else's phone may fall under the Criminal Code: illegal access to a computer system (art. 360) and illegal interception of a computer data transmission (art. 361). Prosecutors and the court decide the actual classification.

Signs of spyware and what to do

The useful signs are mostly in the accounts: logins from unfamiliar places or devices, unknown active sessions, forwarding rules that appeared in the mailbox, compromised-password alerts. On the device, weaker hints are battery or mobile data used for no reason, unknown apps and unknown configuration or management profiles on the phone.

If you suspect an infostealer: change passwords from a different device, not the suspect one; invalidate all sessions; check two-factor settings and mailbox rules; then clean or reinstall the workstation.

Prevention: software only from official sources, updates, endpoint detection and response on workstations, which can catch suspicious collection and transmission even from new variants, a password manager instead of browser-saved passwords, and two-factor authentication everywhere.

Employee monitoring: the legal line

Law 190/2018, art. 5, allows monitoring through electronic communications or video at the workplace only if: the employer's legitimate interest is soundly justified and prevails; employees were informed in advance, fully and explicitly; the union or representatives were consulted beforehand; less intrusive methods did not work; and data is kept proportionately, for at most 30 days, with the exceptions set by law.

In Bărbulescu v. Romania (ECtHR, Grand Chamber, 5 September 2017, application 61496/08), an employer had read an employee's Yahoo Messenger messages. The Court found, by eleven votes to six, a violation of Article 8 (private life and correspondence), because the Romanian courts had not checked whether the employee had been warned in advance about the nature and extent of the monitoring.

For a company: a tool that captures keystrokes, screens or the camera „so that employees will not know” usually breaches the prior-information condition and, depending on the facts, may carry criminal liability (art. 361 of the Criminal Code or, for audio or video recording in private spaces, art. 226). Lawyer and data protection officer first.

Frequently asked questions

How would I know I have spyware if it produces no symptoms?

Rarely from the machine itself — the useful signs live in the accounts: logins from unfamiliar cities or devices, which the security pages of the major providers list; unknown active sessions; forwarding rules that appeared in the mailbox; alerts from services about compromised passwords. Add periodic scans with serious endpoint tooling. The correct reflex at any signal inverts instinct: assume the machine you are working on is compromised and change the passwords from a different device.

An employee installed a free program and foreign logins appeared next day — what is the connection?

Most likely an infostealer bundled with pirated or counterfeit software: browser passwords and session cookies were sold or used within hours. Response: isolate the machine, change passwords from a clean device, invalidate sessions, check mailbox rules and two-factor settings, reinstall. Policy: restricted install rights and an approved tool list.

Is it legal to monitor what employees do on company computers?

Yes, if you meet Law 190/2018 art. 5: a justified legitimate interest, full prior information of employees, consultation of the union or representatives, no less intrusive method available, and data kept at most 30 days. Covert monitoring usually breaches these conditions and can also have criminal consequences; consult a lawyer first.

What does the word spyware mean?

Spyware means a spy program: the word joins spy and software. It describes software installed in secret that collects and sends data from a computer or phone. In Romanian it is called „program spion”, and the English term is used as is, written spyware-ul with a hyphen when the definite article is added.

How can I tell if I have spyware on my phone?

Signs: battery and mobile data used for no reason, a warm phone at rest, unknown apps, administration profiles or accessibility access granted to an unknown app, accounts accessed from foreign devices. On an iPhone, update iOS and check profiles under Settings, General, VPN and Device Management. With stalkerware, removal can alert the person who installed it.

Does antivirus detect spyware?

Largely yes: current antivirus and endpoint detection tools detect infostealers, keyloggers and many stalkerware apps, but not everything; new or mercenary spyware can pass unnoticed. That is why account activity (logins, sessions) matters, and why a password manager with two-factor authentication makes the harvest far less valuable.
