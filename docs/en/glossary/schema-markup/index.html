What is schema markup?

Structured data added to a page's code, using the schema.org vocabulary, that tells search engines exactly what the page contains: a product, a company, an article or an event.

Schema markup (also called structured data or schema.org markup) is a block of code added to a page, usually in JSON-LD format, that tells search engines what the page contains: a product with price and stock, a company with an address and opening hours, an article, an event. Visitors do not see it and the design stays unchanged. The vocabulary comes from schema.org, founded by Google, Microsoft, Yahoo and Yandex; the current release is 30.1, from 16.09.2026, and over 45 million domains used it in 2024. Since 07.05.2026 Google no longer shows FAQ results at all, however correct the markup; below is what still works.

For a company the stake is simple. Without markup, Google infers from the text what is on the page; with markup it receives the facts outright: this is a product at 249 lei, in stock, rated 4.8 across 320 customer reviews; this is a company at this address with these opening hours. Valid markup makes the page eligible for rich results, but Google does not guarantee they will show. The markup lives in the site templates, so a redesign can remove it without anyone noticing.

Why it matters for your business

Rich results in Google

Price, stock and rating for products, dates for events, job postings, the navigation trail (desktop only since January 2025). Google's case studies: Rotten Tomatoes +25% clicks, Nestlé +82% for pages shown as rich results. Eligibility, not a guarantee.

Google identifies the company correctly

With Organization or LocalBusiness, the legal name, logo, address, hours and contact can reach the knowledge panel. The data must match your Google Business profile and the visible text on the page.

The same data, no contradictions

When the page and the markup are fed from one source (catalog or CMS), the price, stock and hours in Google match the site. Mismatched or hidden data makes the page ineligible for rich results.

What schema markup looks like: JSON-LD, Microdata and RDFa

Google accepts three formats: JSON-LD, Microdata and RDFa, all equally fine for it, but it recommends JSON-LD. That is a <script type="application/ld+json"> block kept apart from the visible HTML, so it can be added or removed without touching the design. Google reads it even when JavaScript injects it.

A block always has @context (schema.org), @type (Product, for example) and properties: name, then offers with price, priceCurrency (RON) and availability. Fill in every property the type requires; Google prefers fewer properties that are complete and accurate over many optional ones.

Useful structured data types for a company

Pick the most specific type that matches the main content of the page. Organization suits the home or about page. LocalBusiness fits companies with a physical location and opening hours. Product with Offer describes price and stock, and AggregateRating is added only from real customer reviews. Others: Article or BlogPosting for articles, BreadcrumbList for the navigation trail, Event for events, JobPosting for job openings.

FAQPage stays a valid type and does no harm, but it has produced no visible result in Google since 07.05.2026.

What Google removed from results between 2023 and 2026

Google retires result types periodically. HowTo disappeared first on mobile (August 2023) and then on desktop (13.09.2023). FAQ was limited on 08.08.2023 to well-known government and health sites, and since 07.05.2026 it is not shown at all. In 2025 five more types were withdrawn: course info, estimated salary, learning video, special announcement and vehicle listing. Breadcrumbs have appeared on desktop only since 22.01.2025.

The practical lesson: do not pay for markup whose only goal is a result Google no longer shows. Unused markup does no harm, Google said in 2023, so there is no need to delete it.

Google's rules: what you may not mark up

Mark up only content visitors can see, and put the markup on the page it describes. Do not mark up fake reviews or content unrelated to the page.

Stars for your own company are not allowed: since 16.09.2019 a business marked up as LocalBusiness or Organization gets no stars when it controls the reviews, including when it embeds a Google or Facebook review widget on its site. Since 24.07.2026 Google also bans fake reviews and undisclosed incentivised ones (money, discounts, vouchers, free products), on the page or in the markup. A breach can bring a manual action: the page loses eligibility for rich results, but its ranking in search does not change.

What to check with your site provider

Ask for markup generated from the same data as the page (catalog, CMS), not pasted by hand, so it does not break at the first price change. Ask for a pass in the Rich Results Test and in validator.schema.org, and clean rich-result reports in Search Console. Ask for a re-check after every template change or redesign.

Walk away from promises of FAQ results in Google, of stars for your own company, and of "position 1 with schema markup": the first two are no longer possible and the third does not exist.

Frequently asked questions

Which schema types are worth implementing first?⌄

LocalBusiness and Organization for company details (address, hours, contact), Product with Offer and AggregateRating for shops (from real customer reviews only), Article for articles and BreadcrumbList for navigation. For services, schema.org has a Service type, but Google has no rich result for it; the company shows through Organization or LocalBusiness. FAQPage no longer produces visible results.

Does schema markup improve rankings?⌄

Not directly. Structured data is not a declared ranking factor; it makes the page eligible for rich results, and Google does not guarantee they will show. When they do, they take more space and can earn more clicks at the same position, as Google's published case studies show.

How do I check whether my structured data is correct?⌄

Paste the page address into Google's Rich Results Test, which reports the types detected for Google features and any errors. For any schema.org markup use validator.schema.org. The rich-result reports in Search Console show problems across the whole site.

Do FAQ results still appear in Google?⌄

No. Since 07.05.2026 Google no longer shows FAQ results, and from 08.08.2023 it showed them only for government and health sites. FAQPage markup can stay on the page and does no harm, but it brings no visible result.

Can I show star ratings for my own company in Google?⌄

No, if the reviews about your company are shown on your own site, directly or through a Google or Facebook widget: LocalBusiness and Organization get no stars. Products can carry a rating from real customer reviews. Fake or undisclosed incentivised reviews are banned.

Do I need structured data to appear in AI Overviews or ChatGPT?⌄

No, Google says in its AI optimization guide (May 2026): structured data is not required for AI features in Search and there is no special markup for them. It stays useful for rich results. ChatGPT and Perplexity have published no rule about schema.org.

What is the difference between schema markup and structured data?⌄

Structured data is any information organised in a standard format. Schema markup is structured data written in the schema.org vocabulary, used by Google, Bing and Yandex. In SEO the two terms are used as synonyms. Do not confuse them with structured data in databases, from computer science.
