What is conversion rate and how is it calculated?

The percentage of visitors who take the tracked action — buy, request a quote, call: conversions ÷ visitors × 100, the figure connecting traffic to money.

Conversion rate is the percentage of visitors who take the action you track on your site — an order, a quote request, a booking, a phone call — calculated as conversions divided by visitors, multiplied by 100: 30 orders from 1,500 visits is 2%. Two online shops receive the same traffic, yet one sells twice as much as the other; the difference lies here. The arithmetic is simple: at the same promotion budget, doubling conversion doubles sales, without spending more on ads. That turns the question from how do we bring more traffic into how do we convert the traffic already paid for.

What you count as a conversion decides what the figure is worth. A macro conversion is the action that brings money directly (an order, a qualified quote request); micro conversions are the small steps on the way — a newsletter signup, a wishlist, a created account — which predict and feed the big sale. Define conversion by real value: a qualified quote request, not a visit longer than 30 seconds, because vanity metrics only make reports look good. Below: the formula, where the figure appears in GA4 and Google Ads, dated benchmarks and the improvement cycle.

Why it matters for your business

✓Extra sales from existing traffic

Conversion work extracts more revenue from the same visitors — growth without increasing the advertising budget at all.

✓An exact diagnosis of the losses

A funnel measured step by step shows precisely where sales die — at the basket, at delivery, at payment — and puts repairs in order of money.

✓Every change verified against numbers

Redesigns and improvements are judged on conversion before and after rather than on taste; the site then evolves on evidence.

The conversion rate formula, with examples

Conversion rate = (conversions ÷ visitors) × 100. For a shop: 30 orders from 1,500 visits give 2%. For a service site: 6 quote requests from 200 visitors give 3%.

The denominator changes the figure, so always say what you divide by: users, sessions or ad clicks. The same 30 orders from 1,500 sessions give 2%; if the sessions come from 1,000 users, the rate per user is 3%. Compare only figures calculated the same way.

In a shop, also calculate the rate of each funnel step. Example: of 4,000 visitors, 800 view a product (20%), 160 add to basket (20% of the 800), 80 start payment (50%) and 48 complete the order (60%). The total rate is 48 ÷ 4,000 = 1.2%, the product of the step rates. The step with the biggest loss shows where to repair first.

On small samples the figure jumps: 1 order from 20 visits is 5%, and one more makes it 10%. Judge by whole months of data, not days.

Where you read it: GA4 and Google Ads

In Google Analytics 4, the important actions on a site are called key events. The word conversion is kept for actions you use in Google Ads campaigns (Google Analytics help, Key events page, checked 18.11.2026).

GA4 offers two rates for the same site: session key event rate (sessions with a key event ÷ total sessions) and user key event rate (users who completed a key action ÷ total users). The figures differ because the denominators differ.

In Google Ads, conversion rate means conversions ÷ total ad interactions that can be tracked to a conversion in the same period: 50 conversions from 1,000 interactions give 5%. If you track several conversion actions or count Every conversion, one click can produce more than one conversion and the rate can exceed 100% (Google Ads help, checked 18.11.2026). A suddenly very high rate usually points to a duplicated event or one marked as a key event too broadly.

What conversion rate is good: dated, sourced benchmarks

For Romanian online shops, the latest named dataset is the GPeC E-Commerce Romania Report 2023 (published 17.05.2024): indicative statistics from Limitless Agency, based on about 400 of the agency's e-commerce clients. Examples: IT&C 0.9%, Industrial 1%, Fashion 1.24%, Sport 1.5%, Gifts 2.27%, Food 2.43%, Beauty 5.14%. Most categories sit between 0.9% and 2.6%. It is one agency's figures, from 2023.

For landing pages with a form or an offer, Unbounce (Conversion Benchmark Report 2024, over 41,000 pages) gives a median of 6.6%, with industry medians between 3.8% and 12.3%.

The decision rule stays the same: your own history beats every benchmark. Compare this month with three months ago, by segment (mobile apart from desktop, by traffic source), because averages hide exactly the problems.

Conversion rate, CTR and bounce rate

CTR (clicks ÷ impressions) shows what happens before the visit; conversion rate shows what happens after it. The details on CTR are in its own term.

GA4 bounce rate is the percentage of sessions without engagement (100% minus engagement rate). It tells how fast people leave, not whether they bought; a page can have a high bounce rate and good conversion.

How to raise conversion rate (CRO)

Conversion rate optimization (CRO) follows one cycle. Measure correctly, with goals defined in analytics, by segment. Diagnose where the journey breaks: the funnel step by step, plus heatmaps and recordings for the why behind the numbers. Form hypotheses about the usual suspects: mobile speed, surprise delivery costs, long forms, missing trust signals, a buried call to action. Test one change at a time, ideally A/B, at least before and after.

For shops, Baymard Institute pooled 50 studies (2006–2025) with an average cart abandonment rate of 70.22% (updated 28.10.2026). Among the reasons given by US shoppers, excluding those just browsing: extra costs too high (shipping, tax) 40%, mandatory account 18%, checkout too long or complicated 17%.

Frequently asked questions

How is conversion rate calculated?

Divide the number of conversions by the number of visitors and multiply by 100. Example: 30 orders from 1,500 visits give 2%. Always state the denominator you use (users, sessions or ad clicks), because the same activity gives different figures.

What is a good conversion rate?

It depends on niche and on how a conversion is defined. For Romanian online shops, GPeC 2023 (Limitless data) shows most categories between 0.9% and 2.6%; for landing pages, Unbounce 2024 gives a median of 6.6%. The best benchmark remains your own figure, by segment, from three months ago.

Can conversion rate exceed 100%?

Yes, in Google Ads, when you track several conversion actions or count Every conversion: one click can produce more than one conversion. In GA4 the session rate cannot exceed 100%, because it counts sessions with at least one key event. If you see absurd figures, check for duplicated events.

Why does conversion rate differ between GA4 and Google Ads?

They use different denominators: GA4 divides by sessions or users, Google Ads by ad interactions. The counted events also differ (key events versus conversions used for bidding), and so does attribution. Compare only figures from the same tool and the same period.

Why does mobile convert worse than desktop?

In Unbounce's 2024 report, 83% of landing page visits came from mobile, and desktop converted 8% better. The culprits are usually concrete: speed, heavy forms on a small screen, tiny buttons, large pop-ups. Test on a mid-range phone over a mobile connection, plus a speed report.

Which improvements usually give the fastest conversion gain?

From Baymard's abandonment reasons: show the total cost early (extra costs, 40%, are the top reason), allow guest checkout (18%) and shorten the checkout (17%). Add trust signals beside the button and improve mobile speed. Verify each change with an A/B test.
