Walk into one and the noise hits first: rows of racks behind steel mesh, cold air pushed up through perforated floor tiles, cooling units that never stop. Behind that sits the engineering: often two grid feeds, diesel generators, battery banks covering the seconds between them, fire suppression that will not drown the hardware, and entry control where every badge swipe is recorded. Whenever a supplier says your system runs in the cloud, this is where it physically ends up: a rented rack, a locked cage, or a virtual slice of someone else's machine inside a facility like that. Three things make this a commercial question rather than a technical curiosity. Jurisdiction decides which authorities can compel access to your records and how comfortable your European customers will be signing with you. Engineering decides whether a heatwave or a substation failure becomes your outage. The contract decides how quickly you can walk away with your data intact.
