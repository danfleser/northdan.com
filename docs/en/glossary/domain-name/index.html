Domain name (internet domain): what it is and who holds it

Your company's address on the internet — yourcompany.com — a right of use paid for periodically, not an owned thing, and one that must be registered in your company's name.

6

7

A domain name (internet domain) is the unique, memorable address of a website, such as company.ro, which the DNS system translates into the IP address of the server where the site lives; for the .ro extension the official registry is RoTLD (ICI Bucharest), which guarantees each name is registered only once worldwide. The same domain carries the company's email (office@company.ro) and the address printed on vehicles and invoices.

Of all the things a company can accidentally not own, its own web address is the most common and the most painful. If your agency, your web developer or a departed employee is listed as the registrant instead of the company, they hold the asset, and transferring it later depends on their goodwill. Check the registrant now, set renewal to automatic, enable transfer locking and two-factor authentication where the registrar offers them, and treat the login as a company credential rather than a personal one.

Why it matters for your business

✓Identity and credibility

A proper company domain on the website and on email signals a real business, where a free mailbox address quietly signals the opposite.

✓An asset that gains value

Search authority, inbound links and years of printed references accumulate against the address, not against the hosting behind it.

✓Independence from suppliers

Owning the registration makes changing agency, developer or host an administrative task rather than a negotiation.

Parts of a domain: name, extension, subdomain

A domain has a name and an extension (TLD): .ro, .com, .eu. Read right to left it has levels: the extension, then the second-level name (company.ro), then any subdomains such as www or blog, which the holder creates freely. The .ro zone also has sub-zones such as .com.ro, .org.ro, .nom.ro, .store.ro and .info.ro.

For .ro specifically, RoTLD allows at most 63 characters before the extension, only letters a–z, digits 0–9 and hyphens (not at the start or end), and ignores letter case. Romanian diacritics have been accepted in .ro names since 2 June 2015. A registered name cannot be changed: any altered character is a new domain, registered separately.

How it works: the browser asks the DNS system which IP address matches the name and opens the server there. DNS changes propagate within 24 hours at most, according to RoTLD.

Domain versus hosting, website and URL

The domain is the address; hosting is the server space holding the files; the website is those files. They can sit with different providers, which is why the domain stays yours when you move the site. A URL is the full address of one page: the https:// protocol, the domain and the page path.

For .ro, a domain registered without declared DNS servers is only reserved: it exists but serves no website and no mailbox, according to RoTLD. After registering, it still has to be pointed at the hosting.

The .ro rules: who registers, for how long, what happens at expiry

Any natural or legal person from any country can register a .ro domain. There is exactly one holder, never two, and a company must already exist when the request is made. First come, first served applies, and the domain becomes active only after the bank confirms payment.

Since 1 March 2018 RoTLD charges registration and annual maintenance, for 1 to 10 years; before that the payment was one-off. At expiry the domain enters the PendingDelete state and stops working, but the holder can still renew or transfer it. Once deleted, the name is free for anyone.

At accredited registrars a .ro costs roughly 40–70 lei a year excl. VAT and a .com about 10–15 € a year; the full breakdown is in the domain pricing guide.

Who owns the domain: the right of use and the holder

Registering a .ro gives only a right of use, not ownership of the name. Only the holder can update contact details and DNS servers; the technical contact has no administration right, and the contact person must be an employee of the holder company, under the RoTLD rules.

Changing the holder is a right-of-use transfer: the current holder generates an authorisation key in RoTLD's online account, the key is sent to the holder's email, the new holder fills in the form and pays, and the expiry date restarts. Moving to another registrar also needs a key sent to the holder's email.

Since January 2025 RoTLD verifies holder data under Romanian emergency ordinance OUG 155/2024: it emails the contact address, and if the data is not validated by the deadline the domain is suspended and scheduled for deletion.

The consequence for a business: the holder's email is the key to everything (transfer, validation, recovery). If it is the agency's or a former employee's address, the company cannot transfer, validate or recover its own domain.

Frequently asked questions

What does a domain cost and where should I register it?

A .com costs about 10–15 € a year and a .ro about 40–70 lei a year excl. VAT at accredited registrars, either directly at RoTLD or through an accredited partner. Buy from an established registrar offering two-factor authentication, transfer locking and support in a language your team speaks. Cheap first-year offers usually renew at several times the headline price, so read the renewal rate.

Does anyone check whether the domain is registered to the company?

Nobody checks it for you, which is exactly why it goes wrong. Look your own domain up in the web WHOIS on rotld.ro: holder data is shown for legal persons and hidden for natural persons, so if no company name appears the domain is probably registered to an individual. Request a holder change while the relationship is still friendly.

Is it worth buying several variants of my domain?

A handful, yes: the obvious alternative extension for your market, the most likely misspelling, and the version with and without a hyphen, all redirecting to the main address. Buying dozens defensively is a subscription to anxiety. Money is better spent registering the trademark, which gives you a recovery route if someone takes a variant in bad faith.

What does domain mean besides the internet?

In Romanian, domeniu also means a field of activity, the set of values a function is defined on (mathematics) and an estate in history, per the dexonline dictionary. On the internet it means a domain name: the address of a website, for example company.ro.

Can a company that does not exist yet register a .ro domain?

No, the holder must exist when the request is made, according to RoTLD. You can wait until the company is incorporated, or register the domain in the founder's name and later do a right-of-use transfer to the company, which needs an authorisation key and a fee.

Can the name of a domain be changed after registration?

No, a registered .ro name cannot be modified, per the RoTLD rules. Any changed character produces a new domain, registered separately. If you want a different name, register the new one and redirect the old one so visitors and links are not lost.

How long until a new domain works?

The domain becomes active after the bank confirms payment, then the hosting's DNS servers must be declared, otherwise it stays only reserved. DNS changes propagate within 24 hours at most, according to RoTLD; paying online by card avoids waiting for a bank transfer to clear.
