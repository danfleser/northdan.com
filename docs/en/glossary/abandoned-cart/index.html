Abandoned cart: meaning, average rate and recovery

An abandoned cart is a basket in which a visitor to an online shop has put products but which they leave without placing the order.

6

On average 70.22% of online shopping carts are abandoned, roughly 7 in 10, according to Baymard Institute's average of 50 studies published 2006–2025 (page updated 28 October 2026). Part of that cannot be recovered: 42% of US online shoppers abandoned a cart because they were just browsing (same Baymard study). The rest have reasons the shop can fix: delivery costs revealed only at the final step, a compulsory account, a form that is too long, or a page that crawls on a phone.

For your business an abandoned cart is an almost-completed sale: the customer already chose the product and stopped one step short of paying. Part of those orders is recoverable through reminder emails, through a simpler checkout and through showing total costs from the start. Baymard estimates that better checkout design can raise the conversion rate of an average large shop by 35.26%. Checked on 18 November 2026 on baymard.com and on the Romanian Legislative Portal.

Why it matters for your business

✓Sales recovered from traffic already paid for

Recovery emails, sent to people who accepted commercial messages, bring back a share of departed customers — additional revenue earned without any increase in the advertising budget.

✓A clear diagnosis of site problems

The exact step where customers give up shows what is blocking them: the shipping charge, the form, the payment options — so you know what to repair first.

✓Margin rises with the completion rate

Every percentage point of baskets carried through means extra orders per month against the same fixed costs, which lands directly in profit.

Cart abandonment rate: formula and average

Abandonment rate = (1 − completed purchases ÷ carts created) × 100. Example: 1,000 carts created and 300 completed orders give a 70% abandonment rate.

The documented average is 70.22%, calculated by Baymard Institute as the unweighted average of 50 studies; the rate in individual studies ranges from 55% to 84%, depending on traffic sources and on how the analysis defines a cart. Baymard cites no comparable study for Romania, so the figure that matters is your own shop's rate, tracked monthly.

In Google Analytics 4, carts created show up in the add_to_cart event and orders in purchase; the begin_checkout – purchase ratio separately shows checkout abandonment. More on the tool: web analytics.

Why customers leave: the measured reasons

Setting aside the 42% who were just browsing, Baymard (US shoppers, multiple answers allowed) counts these reasons: extra costs too high (shipping, taxes, fees) 40%; delivery too slow 20%; did not trust the site with card details 19%; account creation required 18%; checkout too long or complicated 17%; site errors or crashes 17%; unsatisfactory returns policy 13%; total cost not visible upfront 12%; card declined 10%; too few payment methods 9%.

Each reason has a fix: the total cost visible in the cart, a guest-order option, card payment on a page that looks safe, an easy-to-find returns policy, more payment methods. Fields and form length are covered on the checkout page.

What the law requires before the order button

In Romania, OUG 34/2014 art. 6 para. (1) lit. e) requires the total price, with all taxes included and, where relevant, all additional shipping and delivery costs, to be communicated to the consumer. Art. 8 para. (2) requires that, for an online order with a payment obligation, this information be given "in a clear and very visible manner, immediately before the consumer places the order". Art. 8 para. (3) requires the site to show "at the latest at the beginning of the ordering process" any delivery restrictions and the accepted means of payment. This is the Romanian transposition of the EU Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU; the article numbers are Romanian and do not apply abroad.

These are the same reasons as in the previous section: extra costs (40%), total not visible upfront (12%), too few payment methods (9%). A shop that applies the law therefore removes part of its abandonment. The wording of the order button ("order with an obligation to pay") is covered under call to action; the paperwork of an online shop is in what documents you need for an online shop.

The recovery email and consent

In Romania, Law 506/2004 art. 12 para. (1) requires prior express consent for commercial messages by email, SMS or automated calls (the Romanian transposition of the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC). Para. (2) makes an exception only for an address obtained directly from a customer "on the occasion of a sale" of a product, for similar products, with a simple and free way to refuse at collection and in every message. Para. (3) requires an identifiable sender and a valid unsubscribe address. A breach of art. 12 is fined from 5,000 to 100,000 lei, and for companies with a turnover above 5,000,000 lei up to 2% of turnover (art. 13 para. (2)).

Our reading: a visitor who never bought and merely left an address at checkout is not covered by the exception in para. (2). The prudent version is a consent box for commercial messages, not pre-ticked. For a concrete case, ask a data-protection lawyer; the personal-data rules are under GDPR.

In practice: one message the same day, a few hours after the visitor left, and at most one more the next day; a discount only on chosen rules, not on every cart, otherwise customers learn to wait for the discount. For visitors with no address there is remarketing, which needs cookie consent. The sequence can run automatically through marketing automation.

Abandoned cart, abandoned checkout and churn

Baymard counts cart abandonment (products added, order not started) and checkout abandonment (order started, not finished) together. As a rule, the recovery email works only for a checkout in which the customer has already typed an email address. Churn is something else: customers who bought before and stopped buying, measured over long periods. See churn.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main reasons customers abandon their cart?

The most common, per Baymard (US shoppers): extra costs too high 40%, slow delivery 20%, no trust in card payment 19% and a compulsory account 18%. Most are fixed in the cart and on the checkout page (total cost shown early, guest order, a payment step that inspires trust) rather than by cutting prices.

How do cart recovery emails work?

The shop sends a message showing the products still in the basket with a direct link to finish, typically the same day and at most once more the next day, and only to people who accepted commercial messages (Romanian Law 506/2004 art. 12). It can help, but sent for every cart it teaches customers to wait for a discount.

Can I see in numbers how many carts are abandoned on my site?

Yes. Abandonment rate = (1 − completed orders ÷ carts created) × 100. E-commerce platforms show it directly; in GA4, carts created appear in the add_to_cart event and orders in purchase, while begin_checkout – purchase separately shows checkout abandonment. Tracking it monthly tells you whether a change to the site helped or hurt.

What is the average cart abandonment rate?

70.22%, roughly 7 in 10 carts, the average of 50 studies published 2006–2025 (Baymard Institute, updated 28 October 2026). Rates range from 55% to 84% between studies. Of US shoppers, 42% abandoned a cart because they were just browsing.

Can I send an abandoned-cart email without consent?

As a rule, no. Romanian Law 506/2004 art. 12 para. (1) requires prior consent for commercial email. The exception in para. (2) covers only customers who already bought from you, for similar products, with a simple unsubscribe in every message.

What does abandoned cart mean?

An abandoned cart is a basket in which products were placed but the order was never placed. An abandoned checkout is an abandonment after the customer has started the checkout, for example after typing an email or delivery address.
