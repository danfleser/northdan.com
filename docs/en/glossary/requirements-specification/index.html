The requirements specification (caiet de sarcini): definition and contents

The document that fixes what anyone who wants the contract must deliver: in public procurement it is a mandatory part of the tender documentation, in software it is the basis for comparable quotes.

6

7
questions answered

A requirements specification (Romanian: caiet de sarcini) is the document that sets the minimum requirements the commissioned product, service or work must meet, so that every bidder prices the same thing; in Romanian public procurement it is part of the tender documentation and must contain technical specifications (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 20). Besides the specifications, it usually fixes quantities or volumes, deadlines, warranties, the obligations of each party and the acceptance criteria, and once the contract is signed it becomes an annex to it (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 147).

The most expensive words in a software project are we assumed that was obvious, and a requirements specification exists so that nobody has to say them. It does not have to be technical: the most valuable one describes the business, meaning how an order travels through the company today, where time is lost and what has to change. On its basis you receive quotes that can actually be compared, and during the project it arbitrates what is included in the price and what is an additional request. A serious supplier will help you refine it before quoting; a refusal to engage with it at all is a clear warning signal.

Why it matters for your business

✓Comparable quotes instead of guesswork

Every supplier prices the same clearly described object, so price differences become meaningful rather than the by-product of different assumptions.

✓An arbiter for the was-this-included argument

What is written is delivered within the price; what is not written is negotiated separately. Disputes end with a quotation from the document, not with lawyers.

✓Thinking done before spending

Writing the document forces you to clarify how the company actually works, and many businesses discover valuable simplifications before paying for a single line of code.

What a requirements specification contains

It starts with the subject and context: what is being bought and why. Then come the technical specifications, the mandatory part. They can be written as performance or functional requirements, by reference to standards (accompanied by the words "or equivalent"), or as a combination (Law 98/2016, art. 156 para. 1).

Next come the expected quantities or volumes, delivery deadlines and stages, warranties, and the acceptance criteria and method. When the purchase is used by individuals, the specifications must take into account accessibility requirements for people with disabilities, except in duly justified cases (art. 155 para. 4).

For software, the document can also state whether a transfer of intellectual property rights is required (art. 155 para. 3): in practice, the source code and the licence of use.

The caiet de sarcini in Romanian public procurement

The tender documentation has four parts: the DUAE (European Single Procurement Document) and the instructions to bidders, the requirements specification, the draft contract with its mandatory clauses, and the forms and templates (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 20 para. 1). In competitive dialogue, negotiation and innovation partnership, a descriptive document is used instead of the specification.

The contracting authority's specialised internal procurement department drafts the tender documentation or coordinates its drafting, based on the needs sent by the specialist departments (art. 2 para. 3 letter c). Specifications may name a manufacturer, brand or patent only exceptionally, when the subject cannot be described otherwise, and then with "or equivalent" (Law 98/2016, art. 156 paras. 2-3).

After signing, the specification is a mandatory annex to the contract, and if the technical proposal is inferior to it or does not match it, the specification prevails (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 147). A qualification criterion that appears only in the specification and not in the notice is treated as an unwritten clause (art. 30 para. 6).

Specification, technical specifications, tender documentation: the difference

The technical specifications are the mandatory technical part of the document. The requirements specification is the technical specifications plus quantities, deadlines and acceptance. The tender documentation is the whole file: the specification, the draft contract, the forms and the instructions to bidders. The technical offer is the supplier's answer to the specification.

Technical documentation is something else: you receive it at handover, and it describes what was built, not what you asked for.

The requirements specification on a software project

On a software project, each major capability must be described so that it can be verified at delivery. It is just as useful to write what the application does NOT do: stated limits prevent half of the "we thought that was included" arguments.

On a privately funded project, the same document is what makes two quotes comparable. On an EU-funded project, private beneficiaries award contracts under Order 1284/2016, extended to 2021-2027 by MIPE Order no. 1,354/30.03.2023, and the POCIDIF 2.1 file asks for minimum technical specifications for each expense in the cost summary, compared against two offers. The drafting steps are on the page about how to write a specification for an application, and the format the file expects is on the page about technical specifications for software.

Frequently asked questions

How detailed does the requirements specification need to be?

Detailed enough that each major capability is verifiable at delivery. Manages orders is useless; the user sees orders filtered by status and receives a notification when the status changes is verifiable. Fine screen details can be refined during the project — business flows cannot.

Who should write it — us or the supplier?

Ideally both. You bring the processes and business priorities; a consultant or the supplier brings structure and the questions that surface exceptions. A document written entirely by the supplier risks describing what is convenient for them to build.

Does a specification block changes during the project?

No. It fixes the starting point and the mechanism for change: every modification is assessed transparently for time and cost impact. Without a reference document, each change becomes a negotiation conducted from conflicting memories.

Who writes the requirements specification in public procurement?

The specialised internal procurement department drafts it or coordinates its drafting, based on the needs sent by the specialist departments (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 2 para. 3 letter c).

What must a requirements specification contain?

Technical specifications: the requirements and characteristics that let the product, service or work be described objectively (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 20 para. 10). In practice quantities, deadlines, warranties and acceptance criteria are added so that offers can be compared.

Can a requirements specification demand a specific brand or product?

No, except exceptionally, when the subject cannot be described otherwise, and then with the words "or equivalent" (Law 98/2016, art. 156 paras. 2-3). The rule keeps the market open: any bidder whose product meets the requirements must be able to compete.

What happens if the technical offer does not follow the requirements specification?

In the contract, the specification prevails (Government Decision 395/2016, art. 147 para. 2). At evaluation, the technical proposal must meet the minimum requirements in the specification, otherwise the offer risks being declared non-compliant.
