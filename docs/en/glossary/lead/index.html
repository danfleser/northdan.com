What is a lead (sales lead) in sales and marketing?

A lead is a potential customer: a person or company that has shown interest in your offer and left contact details, but has not bought yet.

A lead is a person or company that has shown interest in your products or services and left a way to be reached — filled in a form, asked for a quote, called or subscribed — but has not bought yet. The Romanian equivalent is „client potențial”. A lead becomes a prospect (a qualified lead) once you have confirmed a real need, a budget and decision power, and a customer only after signing or paying. The details left in a form let you answer the request (GDPR art. 6(1)(b)), but not add the person to a newsletter: in Romania, Law 506/2004 art. 12 requires their prior express consent for commercial e-mails and text messages, and the rule also covers company addresses.

Two more things matter for your business. Speed: in a Harvard Business Review study published in 2011, covering 1.25 million leads at 42 US companies, leads contacted within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to be qualified as leads contacted an hour later. And record-keeping: leads held in a salesperson’s head or in a spreadsheet get lost, whereas a CRM with an owner, a stage and a next step with a deadline shows at any moment how many enquiries exist and who was supposed to call back yesterday.

Why it matters for your business

✓The marketing budget becomes measurable

Counting leads per channel and what each costs shows which campaigns bring real enquiries and which merely consume money — and you reallocate accordingly.

✓Predictable sales instead of accidental ones

A clear record of leads by stage shows how many enquiries become proposals and how many become contracts — you can forecast next month rather than hope for it.

✓No enquiry falls between chairs

Every lead has an owner and a next step with a deadline; requests stop vanishing into personal inboxes and notes stuck to a monitor.

Contact, lead, prospect, opportunity and customer

Five words describe the same journey, and companies often mix them up. A contact is a name with a way to communicate, without any sign of interest: for example a phone number from a purchased list. A lead is a contact who has shown interest — asked for a quote, downloaded a guide, called.

A prospect is a qualified lead: you have confirmed they need what you sell and can buy it. Qualification usually follows a commonly used framework, BANT: budget, authority (who decides), need and timing. An opportunity is a prospect with a proposal in progress, a clear need and a clear value. A customer has bought.

Each company writes its definition in one place, so marketing and sales count the same thing. To marketing a lead is often just a completed form; to sales it may only be a genuine quote request. If the definitions differ, the numbers stop matching.

Types of leads

By source, leads are inbound (they came to you: form, phone, chat), outbound (you contacted them first) or referrals. By temperature they are cold (they barely know you), warm (they have interacted with your content) or hot (they asked for a quote or an appointment).

By qualification, marketing and sales use two labels. An MQL (marketing qualified lead) follows your content and fits the customer profile but has not asked for a quote yet. An SQL (sales qualified lead) is a lead whose need sales has confirmed. The move from MQL to SQL is the moment marketing hands the lead to sales. Scoring leads with points automatically is called lead scoring; see the marketing automation page for details.

Producing leads, lead generation, usually starts with a landing page and a clear call to action. All that matters here is knowing they are the source; how you build them is a different subject.

What you may do with a lead’s data

In Romania, answering the request — the quote, the call, the appointment — rests on GDPR art. 6(1)(b): processing is necessary for „steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract”. You do not need a tick-box to send the quote that was asked for.

Newsletters, text messages and promotional e-mails afterwards are different. Law 506/2004 art. 12(1), which transposes art. 13 of the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, requires the recipient’s prior express consent. The exception in art. 12(2) covers only an e-mail address obtained from a customer at the time of a sale, for similar products: a lead who has not bought is outside it. Art. 12(4) extends the rule to subscribers that are legal persons, so B2B is not exempt either.

Valid consent looks like this: a separate, unticked box (recital 32 of the GDPR says pre-ticked boxes do not constitute consent), with the request clearly distinguishable from the rest of the form (art. 7(2)). You must be able to prove the consent (art. 7(1)): keep the date, the form text and the address. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving it (art. 7(3)), and the person is informed at collection (art. 13).

Also check that the address belongs to the person who filled in the form. In October 2025 the Romanian data protection authority (ANSPDCP) fined an operator, Cucina di Fabio S.R.L. (press release of 26.11.2025), 5,084 lei for sending marketing e-mails without proof of consent (art. 6(1)(a)) and 10,169 lei for not first checking that the addresses came from its users and that they had consented (art. 25). In practice, a confirmation e-mail before the first campaign (double opt-in) answers exactly that finding.

When someone says „stop contacting me”, their data can no longer be used for marketing (GDPR art. 21(2)–(3)). ANSPDCP fined Dormeo Home SRL twice in 2026 for marketing calls and text messages continued after an objection: 5,236 lei (press release of 16.06.2026) and 10,517 lei (press release of 08.09.2026). Your CRM should hold a „marketing objection” field and respect it in every campaign.

Purchased lists, the fines under Law 506/2004 and the rules for automated sequences are explained on the marketing automation page.

Response speed and lead records in a CRM

Harvard Business Review published „The Short Life of Online Sales Leads” in March 2011. An audit of 2,241 US companies showed that 37% responded within an hour and 23% never responded; those that responded within 30 days took 42 hours on average. A second study, of 1.25 million leads at 29 B2C and 13 B2B US companies, measured qualification (a real conversation with a decision maker): leads contacted within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to be qualified as leads contacted an hour later, and over 60 times as likely as leads contacted after 24 hours or more. The data is American and over ten years old, but the direction is clear: delay costs.

A CRM helps you not to be late: every lead has an owner from the first minute, the source noted (with UTM parameters), a stage, a next step with a deadline and, if lost, a reason. Without these fields you cannot find out where enquiries are lost.

Three formulas show whether leads bring money. Lead-to-customer rate = customers ÷ leads × 100. Cost per lead (CPL) = campaign cost ÷ leads. Cost per customer = CPL ÷ lead-to-customer rate. Worked example, with round numbers chosen only for the arithmetic: a 2,000 lei campaign brings 40 leads, of which 6 become customers. CPL = 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 lei; rate = 6 ÷ 40 = 15%; cost per customer = 50 ÷ 0.15 ≈ 333 lei.

There is no universal benchmark for the lead-to-customer rate. Compare your own figures month to month and by source: a source with a low CPL and a low rate can cost more per customer than a pricier but better-qualified one.

Frequently asked questions

What does lead mean in sales?

A lead is a potential customer: a person or company that showed interest and left contact details. The English word also means „to guide” (verb) and a metal (lead); in sales and marketing it only has the potential-customer sense. The plural is simply leads.

What is the difference between a lead, a prospect and a customer?

A lead has shown interest and left contact details; a prospect is a qualified lead with a confirmed need and budget; an opportunity is a prospect with a proposal in progress; a customer has signed or paid. Each step between stages has its own conversion rate.

Can I send a newsletter to a lead who asked for a quote?

Not without their separate express consent: the quote request lets you answer it (GDPR art. 6(1)(b)), but promotional e-mails need prior consent under Romanian Law 506/2004 art. 12(1), company addresses included. Add an unticked box for news to your form.

How fast do I have to respond to a new lead?

As fast as possible, ideally within the hour: the study published in Harvard Business Review (2011, 1.25 million leads, US companies) found that firms that tried to make contact within an hour qualified the lead nearly 7 times as often as those that came back an hour later. Set a written response-time rule.

How do I calculate how many leads become customers?

Divide the customers won by the leads received in the same period and from the same source, then multiply by 100. There is no universal benchmark across industries; compare your own rate month to month and by source, and look for the cause of each drop: source, response speed or the offer.

What do MQL and SQL mean?

An MQL (marketing qualified lead) follows your content and fits the profile, without having asked for a quote; an SQL (sales qualified lead) is a lead whose need and willingness to discuss an offer sales has confirmed. Moving from MQL to SQL is the handover from marketing to sales.
